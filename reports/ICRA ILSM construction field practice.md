# Build the Daily Walk, Not Another Form

On US healthcare construction and renovation jobs, the hospital owns ICRA and ILSM: it runs the risk assessment, sets the precaution class, chooses which life safety measures apply, and controls every release gate. The contractor's job is to carry out the controls and produce the proof, usually as paper. That proof is a posted permit, a daily containment log with pressure readings, a daily egress log, fire watch and hot-work paperwork, and corrective-action records. Surveyors from CMS, the Joint Commission (now under Accreditation 360), and state agencies ask for exactly this evidence. In practice the controls are layered: ASHE ICRA 2.0 crosses construction activity Types A–D with four patient risk groups to assign Classes I–V. CDC asks for negative airflow to be checked and documented daily. Joint Commission ILSM, renumbered PE.03.02.01 in 2026, is a policy-driven menu of measures run on clocks: fire watch after 4 hours of fire alarm outage or 10 hours of sprinkler outage, exit inspections every day, and temporary systems tested monthly. For your product, the main point is that the unit of compliance is the permit for each zone plus its recurring logs. A static nested checklist is the wrong unit. The workflow is a role-gated state machine whose final signatures belong to hospital staff. That means the app has to serve two parties even if the contractor pays. It needs a configurable, versioned matrix and measure library for each owner. And it needs offline, append-only evidence capture. The incumbents (Soleran, INCUE, Oxmaint, Veoci, CMMS modules) sell to hospitals. No tool found here owns the contractor's combined ICRA, ILSM, and firestop daily walk, and that is the opening. One limitation applies to everything below: the network policy blocked every primary document and every YouTube transcript. All findings come from search-engine excerpts, so specific matrix cells, thresholds, and standard numbers must be checked against the source PDFs before they are built into the product.

## The hospital owns the risk; the contractor produces the proof

Six sources of authority stack on top of each other, and each one asks for different evidence. **FGI Guidelines** make the ICRA part of the Safety Risk Assessment. The 2018 text, as quoted in a carpenters' training deck, reads: "An infection control risk assessment shall be part of integrated facility planning, design, construction, and commissioning activities" ([Carpenters ITF deck](https://www.mwhcec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Technician-Training-ICRA-Best-Practices-in-Health-Care-Construction.pdf)). In September 2026 FGI renamed its documents the "FGI Facility Code" and moved guidance-type content into separate handbooks ([Healthcare Design Magazine](https://healthcaredesignmagazine.com/?p=165444)). **ASHE ICRA 2.0** (2022, with a permit revised January 2023) is the de facto field tool. It is best practice, not regulation, unless a hospital's policy or contract adopts it ([ASHE ICRA 2.0 permit](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2023/01/ASHE_ICRA_2_TM_permit.pdf)). **CDC/HICPAC 2003** calls for "daily monitoring and documenting of the presence of negative airflow within the construction zone" ([CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)). **CMS** requires the infection prevention program to monitor "repair, renovation and construction activities" under 42 CFR 482.42. CMS also adopts NFPA 101 and NFPA 99 (2012 editions) under 482.41(b) ([CMS SOM Appendix A](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/som107ap_a_hospitals.pdf); [42 CFR 482.41](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/482.41)). **The Joint Commission** required a pre-construction risk assessment (EC.02.06.05) and ILSM (LS.01.02.01) through 2025. **Accreditation 360**, effective January 1, 2026, merged both chapters into a Physical Environment (PE) chapter and cut hospital EC/LS elements of performance by about **48%**. The Joint Commission says the substance did not change ([ASHE](https://www.ashe.org/joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates); [TJC A360 FAQs](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/accreditation/accreditation-360/faqs)). **NFPA 241, 51B and 25** govern construction fire prevention, hot work, and sprinkler impairments.

Every source assigns ownership the same way. Completing the ICRA is "the responsibility of the health care organization requesting or contracting the work," and that organization enforces and updates the precautions ([King PM](https://kingpm.net/infection-control-pitfalls-in-healthcare-construction/)). The infection preventionist (IP) sets requirements and verifies them but does not tell the contractor how to do the work ([Infection Control Today](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/from-blueprints-to-ribbon-cutting-infection-preventionists-role-in-construction)). Under NFPA 241 (2022), the owner designates a **Fire Prevention Program Manager** who runs the fire-prevention plan, hot-work permits, and impairments ([Engineering Fire Protection](https://www.engineeringfireprotection.com/post/major-updates-to-nfpa-241-2022-edition)). Under NFPA 25, an **impairment coordinator** authorizes every preplanned sprinkler impairment ([NFPA 25 Ch. 15 excerpt](https://risk.nv.gov/uploadedfiles/risknvgov/content/Loss_Prevention/Impairments.pdf)). The contractor does the work and keeps the logs. At UVA Health, "the Contractor Representative is responsible for completion of the [Daily Monitoring] log and immediate corrective actions," and confirms negative pressure "at all nonsealed openings into the Work site" ([UVA ICRA Policy 2022](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)).

The sign-off sequence below is reconstructed from owner policies (UVA, UC Davis, Johns Hopkins, NYU, UNC, Cleveland Clinic) and from consultant summaries of ASHE. It is the backbone of the app.

| Stage | Performed by | Signs / approves | App object |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Screening: decide whether PCRA, ICRA and/or ILSM is needed ([NYU policy](https://nyulangone.org/files/exhibit-g2-nyuwuh-construction-process-policy.pdf)) | Owner PM / facilities | Owner | Project intake |
| 2. ICRA class assignment (Type × risk group, adjacent areas, special hazards) | Multidisciplinary team: IP, facilities, safety, clinical, design, contractor | IP + facilities (required for Class III/Type C, IV, V) | ICRA permit v1 |
| 3. ILSM assessment: which measures apply, triggers | Hospital safety officer | Safety officer / FPPM | ILSM assessment |
| 4. Contractor acceptance, permit posted, workers oriented | GC superintendent | GC + each worker (acknowledgment) | Permit acknowledgment, training roster |
| 5. Barrier erection, HVAC isolation, pre-start verification | GC builds; facilities isolates HVAC; owner may smoke-test ([JHM 01 35 33](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Procedures.pdf)) | Facilities + IP | Setup checklist (one-off) |
| 6. Daily containment + pressure + egress walk | GC super / contractor rep | Super (co-sign); owner rep reviews | Recurring logs |
| 7. Periodic rounds (IP weekly or more; hospital safety "daily unless otherwise specified" for ILSM per [NYU Policy 145](https://nyulangone.org/files/policy-145-ils-program.pdf)) | IP, safety, owner rep | Round author; GC signs corrective action | Round + deficiencies |
| 8. Scope / phase change: re-classify, re-assess | Team | IP re-signs | Permit v2 (new version, old kept) |
| 9. Impairment: authorize, tag, notify, fire watch | Impairment coordinator; GC often staffs the watch | Coordinator | Impairment record + fire watch log |
| 10. Closeout gates (next section) | GC, IP, EVS, engineering | Each gate owner in turn | Closeout workflow |

Because the IP, facilities, EVS, and the safety officer all sign inside this workflow, a contractor-only tool cannot complete it. Hospital reviewers need seats, even if those seats are free.

## ICRA 2.0 assigns cumulative controls by class, and the matrix itself varies

ASHE ICRA 2.0 crosses **construction activity Types A–D** (A = inspection and non-invasive work; B = small-scale, short-duration, minimal dust; D = major demolition and construction; the exact Type C wording was not retrievable) with **four patient risk groups** (Low, Medium, High, Highest). The result is **Class of Precautions I–V**. Class V is new in 2.0 ([TX DSHS ASHE presentation](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/IDCU/health/Healthcare-Safety/Conferences/2022/051922-2IP-ICRA2.0-ASHE.pdf); [HFM](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4334-ashe-publishes-revised-infection-control-risk-assessment-guide)). An excerpt of the ASHE-hosted permit gives these cells:

| Risk group | Type A | Type B | Type C | Type D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | I | II | II | III |
| Medium | I | II | III | IV |
| High | I | III | IV | V |
| Highest | III | IV | V | V |

Source: [ASHE ICRA 2.0 Tool + Permit](https://ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/08/ICRA%202.0%20Tool%20%2BPermit.pdf), from a search excerpt. **Treat these cells as unverified.** A CHA sample matrix shows different values (for example, Highest/Type A = II) ([CHA sample](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/ICRA-Construction-Matrix-Sample.pdf)). UC Davis's institutional form adds a Type E column ([UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)). A vendor claims Class II is "now ONLY for maintenance," which does not fit Class II appearing in the Type B and C cells ([Starc Systems](https://starcsystems.com/blog/icra-2-0-healthcare-renovations/)). These conflicts are the strongest case for the app treating the matrix as data rather than code. ASHE also adds a hard override for special hazards: sewage or mold work requires "Class of Precautions IV for LOW and MEDIUM Risk Groups and Class of Precautions V for HIGH and HIGHEST" ([ASHE ICRA 2 edits](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/06/ICRA-2-recent-edits.pdf)). ICRA 2.0 also makes assessing impacts on surrounding areas an explicit step: dust, ventilation, pressurization, noise, vibration, and medical gas ([Accu-Tech](https://www.accu-tech.com/accu-insider/icra-2-what-your-ict-network-designer-and-installer-need-to-know)). That matters because a Japanese prospective study found Aspergillus spores in areas that shared a ceiling void with the renovation and on the floor directly below it ([MDPI 2025-26](https://www.mdpi.com/2036-7449/18/5/101)).

Controls are cumulative: each class includes all the controls of the classes below it. The table below is a **draft template** synthesized from the APIC-hosted matrix, the ASHE form, and the Johns Hopkins and UC Davis permits. Item wording must be reconciled with each owner's adopted permit before release.

| Class | Adds these checklist items (cumulative) | Evidence per item | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup (all) | Permit posted at entry; class and matrix version recorded; adjacent-area check (above, below, beside, shared ceiling void, AIIR/PE rooms, air intakes); signage | Photo of posted permit; signer | Once, and again on each permit version |
| I | Methods that minimize dust; displaced ceiling tile replaced immediately; area cleaned on completion ([APIC matrix](http://apic.org/Resource_/TinyMceFileManager/Education/EPI_Intensive/Resourse_Documents/ICRA_Matrix.pdf)) | Photo after work | Per task |
| II | Water-mist cutting/active dust suppression; unused doors sealed; air vents blocked and sealed or HVAC isolated; removed tiles stored in a clean container or bag; waste contained before transport; walk-off mat (class placement unverified) | Photo of each sealed vent/door; mat change log | Per shift / daily |
| III | **IP-signed permit before start**; HVAC isolated "to prevent contamination of duct system"; critical barriers (sheetrock, plywood, plastic) sealing the work area from the non-work area; taped joints, sealed penetrations; barrier removal only after IP inspection ([APIC matrix](http://apic.org/Resource_/TinyMceFileManager/Education/EPI_Intensive/Resourse_Documents/ICRA_Matrix.pdf)) | IP signature; facilities HVAC signature; photos of top-of-wall and penetrations | Barrier integrity daily |
| IV | HEPA negative air (99.97%) exhausted outdoors, not into recirculating HVAC; barriers to deck meeting NFPA 241; fire-rated penetrations; **continuous pressure device on the containment exterior**; anteroom with HEPA-vac of personnel or coveralls removed on exit ([JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)) | Pressure reading + gauge photo; NAM filter log; smoke-test record | Pressure at least daily (CDC); filters per log |
| V | **Mandatory anteroom** large enough for equipment staging, cart cleaning, and workers; default class for sewage or mold in High/Highest areas ([5D CCS](https://5dccs.com/icra-class-iv-vs-class-v-bay-area-hospital-containment/), vendor; verify against ASHE) | Anteroom photo; all Class IV items | As Class IV |
| Downgrade (III–V) | Inspection and documentation before any downgrade ([JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)) | IP/designee signature | On event |

Closeout is where release authority is clearest and where the order of steps matters most. One practitioner sequence runs: detailed construction clean, IP inspection, EVS terminal clean, second IP inspection, then barrier removal. HVAC isolation is lifted only after IP and engineering release, and negative-air machines keep running until contaminants are flushed ([Healthcare Facility Guide](https://healthcarefacilityguide.com/design/infection-control-summer-construction-icra-protocols/)). UC Davis's permit text reads: "Coordinate an Environmental Services terminal clean... Remove isolation of HVAC system... Do not remove barriers from work area until completed project is inspected by the Infection Preventionist" ([UC Davis](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)). Owners add that "a series of EVS terminal cleans may be required prior to removing the containment, or removal may be phased between cleans," and that the contractor's own declaration of completion must never trigger reoccupancy ([CHA AOEH 2018](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf)). Sources disagree on whether HVAC restoration comes before or after barrier removal. They agree that both wait for IP and engineering release. The default closeout workflow is therefore: **contractor clean and HEPA-vac → IP inspection → EVS terminal clean (repeatable) → IP + engineering release → HVAC isolation removed and pressures verified → barriers removed with HEPA-vac or wet wipe → supply and return registers checked → final photo set → permit closed**. Gate order should be configurable per owner, but no step may be skipped. Water belongs in the closeout as well: lines are flushed and tested before occupancy ([ICT](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/infection-preventionists-role-construction-and-renovation-during-and-after)). Traditional construction ICRA tools "have not expressly included" waterborne-pathogen controls ([PMC9149880](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9149880/)). The 2025 Harlem Legionnaires' outbreak, with **108 cases and 5 deaths**, led to lawsuits naming construction firms including Skanska USA over stormwater in cooling towers next to Harlem Hospital ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/construction-companies-blamed-outbreak-legionnaires-rcna226104); [ABC7 NY](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-lawsuits-filed-nyc-skanska-construction-behalf-victims-harlem-legionnaires-outbreak/17870860/)). Contractors' exposure is no longer hypothetical.

## Pressure readings: no single federal number, so thresholds are per-permit data

No federal rule sets a negative-pressure value for construction containment. The common reference point is the CDC/ASHRAE AII-room differential of **0.01 in. w.c. (2.5 Pa)** ([CDC Air](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/air.html)). Owner documents set their own values: −0.02 in. w.c. on institutional forms, and **>0.03 in. w.g. (7 Pa) for high-risk anterooms** on Northwell's manual log, which also asks for three readings a day with time and initials ([Northwell negative air log](https://www.northwell.edu/sites/northwell.edu/files/2024-01/negative-air-monitoring-log.pdf)). UVA's range came through as −0.01 to −0.05 in. w.c., but the excerpt may be garbled ([UVA](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)). The commonly repeated "−0.03 in. w.c. per FGI/ASHE" attribution could not be verified. CDC's frequency floor is **daily** documentation. Johns Hopkins requires a device "on exterior of work containment to continually monitor" at Class IV ([JHM permit](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)). Barrier integrity is also checked by smoke test, Baulin tube, or pressure alarm ([JHM 01 35 33](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Procedures.pdf)). Connected monitors such as Omni IQ G720 (containment, anteroom, and ambient from one unit), Sonicu, and Spycor already stream readings and alerts ([Omni IQ](https://shop.omnicleanair.com/products/omni-iq-g720-differential-pressure-monitor); [Sonicu](https://www.sonicu.com/differential-air-pressure-solutions)). Omni's claim that ICRA 2.0 "explicitly mandates digital air pressure monitoring" is unverified and should not be repeated.

A pressure reading in the app should be a structured record, not a free-text note. Its fields: permit ID; containment and opening ID (UVA reads at *each* unsealed opening); device ID and calibration date; value with units (in. w.c. or Pa) and a fixed sign convention, negative meaning airflow into the containment; the threshold inherited from the permit; pass/fail computed by the app; method (continuous display, logger download, or smoke/tissue visual); device capture time and server receipt time; reader identity and signature; a gauge photo; alarm state; and, on failure, a corrective action, time restored, and automatic notice to the IP and facilities. The default schedule should be at least once per shift. Extra readings should be triggered by an alarm, a barrier change, an HVAC change, or a restart after a weekend. Owners should be able to configure both. Logger CSV and PDF exports should attach to the day's log, so the app complements the hardware instead of competing with it.

## ILSM is a policy-driven menu running on clocks

Under the Joint Commission, the hospital's own written ILSM policy decides "when and to what extent" each measure applies (EP 1). Since 2023 that policy must also spell out the assessment process ([JC EC News 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf); [HFM](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4618-heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-joint-commissions-ec-and-ls-updates)). ILSM applies when Life Safety Code deficiencies cannot be corrected right away and during construction. Under Accreditation 360 it now sits at **PE.03.02.01** ([TAHFM 2026, Mills](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.tahfm.org/resource/resmgr/annual_conference/2026/doc_mills_george115tuesday.pdf)), and consultants say the content is unchanged apart from the numbers ([EH&E](https://eheinc.com/insights/blog/the-joint-commission-physical-environment-survey-process-2025-and-beyond/)). ILSM is a Joint Commission concept. CMS enforces the underlying Life Safety Code through K-tags. **K-346** requires AHJ notification and evacuation or a fire watch when a fire alarm is out of service **more than 4 hours in 24**. **K-354** does the same for sprinklers at **more than 10 hours in 24** ([CMS-2786W](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/Downloads/CMS2786w.pdf)). States can be stricter: Missouri uses 4 hours for sprinklers ([MO DHSS](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/archives/12905)). A fire watch person must be trained and "cannot have any other assigned duties" ([MO DHSS](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/archives/12905)). One state hospital's form requires hourly surveillance rounds ([Whiting 2018](https://www.cga.ct.gov/ph/tfs/20190426_CVH%20Whiting%20Task%20Force/20190617_WFH%20Operational%20Procedure%20Manual/2018%20WFH%20Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20Form%20With%20Appendices.pdf)). APIC adds a precedence rule: "the ILSMs will override the infection control risk assessment if impairments are present" ([APIC preview](https://apic.org/Resource_/store/books/preview/SLS9808_Preview.pdf)).

The measure library below synthesizes the Joint Commission menu and hospital policies (NYU, Johns Hopkins, Vanderbilt, UNM, Cleveland Clinic, Whiting). Exact EP numbering under PE.03.02.01 is unverified, so labels should not carry EP numbers without a mapping layer.

| # | Measure | Frequency / trigger | Evidence | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | ILSM assessment; measures selected per policy | Before work; redo on scope or phase change ([Cleveland Clinic SOP](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/files/org/supply-chain/non-employee-onboarding/appendix-m-interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm-standard-operating-procedure.pdf?la=en)) | Signed assessment | Hospital safety |
| M2 | Fire department notification + fire watch | Alarm >4 h/24 h; sprinkler >10 h/24 h (many policies start immediately) | Notification times, watch log | Hospital; GC often staffs |
| M3 | Alternative-exit signage | Each egress change; verified daily | Photo per sign | GC installs |
| M4 | **Exit inspection in affected areas** | **Daily** ([JC EC News](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf)) | Per-exit pass/fail, photo on exception | GC super and/or hospital |
| M5 | Temporary equivalent alarm/detection | During impairment | Device locations, test | Hospital / vendor |

| M7 | Additional extinguishers + training on their use | Setup; monthly tag check | Extinguisher ID, tag photo | GC |
| M8 | Partitions smoke-tight or noncombustible / limited-combustible | At erection; daily | Barrier photos, material data sheet | GC builds, hospital inspects |

Two other permit families come with ILSM, and they cover the findings surveyors cite most often. **Hot work** follows NFPA 51B. The post-work fire watch is at least **1 hour** under the current edition according to one source, 30 minutes according to another, and 2 hours after torch roofing in Massachusetts ([LifeSafetyWiki](https://www.lifesafetywiki.com/wiki/nfpa-241); [US Made Supply](https://usmadesupply.com/resources/guides/fire-watch-requirements); [Fast Fire Watch](https://fastfirewatchguards.com/nfpa-241-fire-watch-requirements-for-construction-sites/)). The duration therefore has to be configurable. In hospitals, bypassing smoke detectors for hot work is itself an impairment. **Above-ceiling and barrier-penetration permits** are hospital best practice, not code. Northwell's daily above-the-ceiling permit is "valid for one shift only," requires attestation of certified firestop, and closes on a checklist: penetrations sealed with UL-listed assemblies, systems supported, tiles restored ([Northwell permit](https://www.northwell.edu/sites/northwell.edu/files/2024-01/daily-above-the-ceiling-work-permit.pdf)). This is the highest-value evidence in the product. From January to August 2024 the most frequently scored Joint Commission EC/LS elements were sprinkler items (**LS.02.01.35 EP 4, 55.1**) and **wall penetrations (LS.02.01.10 EP 14, 48.7)**, with units probably % of surveys ([Mills, NEHES 2024](https://cdn.ymaws.com/nehes.org/resource/resmgr/conference/2024/doc_mills_george100324.pdf)). Those deficiencies are created by MEP and low-voltage trades and are hidden once the ceiling closes. So photos of each penetration, with the listed system number, must be taken before closure.

## Eleven records make up the survey-ready file

Surveyors ask for a predictable bundle. During active construction they want the written ILSM plan, fire-watch and drill logs showing the measures actually happened, and staff who can describe them ([CASRAI ILSM](https://casrai.org/guides/interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm)). The CMS worksheet asks whether an ICRA was completed before work began ([CMS IC worksheet](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/provider-enrollment-and-certification/surveycertificationgeninfo/downloads/survey-and-cert-letter-15-12-attachment-1.pdf)). Joint Commission surveyors plan their building tour from life safety drawings that mark construction areas ([TJC Survey Activity Guide](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/2f21045af9d84f5fbc9bfef10d620469?v=04bccd56)). The permit, daily checklists, and HEPA logs must be physically on site ([CHA AOEH](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf)). Treating the risk assessment as static rather than "living" is a common citation ([LifeSafetyWiki](https://www.lifesafetywiki.com/wiki/lsra-ilsm)). A Type B job that becomes Type C work without a new sign-off is "a documented, recurring compliance gap" ([CASRAI ICRA](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)). The minimum fields below come from forms at ASHE, Johns Hopkins, UC Davis, Northwell, Vanderbilt, NIH, and others. Where an excerpt was incomplete, the fields are inferred and should be confirmed against the actual PDFs.

| Record | Kind | Minimum fields |
|---|---|---|
| ICRA permit | Versioned permit | Permit #, project, building/floor/room/zone, dates, scope, activity type, risk group of work and adjacent areas, class, matrix version, special hazards, required controls, HVAC isolation plan, pressure target, contractor and 24-h contact, IP/facilities/safety signatures, expiry and renewal, re-assessment and downgrade history, closeout signatures |
| Daily ICRA containment log | Recurring (daily or per shift) | Barrier intact including above-ceiling, doors closed and taped, pressure at each opening, NAM running, filter and discharge hose OK, walk-off mat, cleaning, new workers briefed ([UVA](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)), signer, co-sign |
| Pressure log | Recurring + event | As in the pressure section |
| ILSM assessment | Versioned assessment | Trigger (deficiency ID or construction scope), affected areas and drawing, LSC features affected, measures M1–M13 selected, assessor, approvals, re-assessment triggers |
| Daily ILSM / egress log | Recurring (daily) | Each exit pass/fail, signage, lighting, barriers smoke-tight, extinguishers, housekeeping, smoking, corrective action with photo; Vanderbilt's sheet certifies "exits and pathways have been checked on the date indicated" ([VUMC log](https://www.vumc.org/pdc/sites/vumc.org.pdc/files/public_files/ILSM%20DAILY%20EGRESS%20INSPECTION%20LOG%2011-2013.pdf)) |
| Impairment record | Event with clock | System and zone, planned vs. emergency, authorizing coordinator, start and expected duration, tags at FDC and control valve (photo), notifications to AHJ/FD, monitoring company, insurer, and house supervisor with times ([Kord Fire](https://kordfire.com/nfpa-25-fire-protection-system-impairment-procedures/)), compensating measures, rolling 24-h counter, restoration test and notices |
| Fire watch log | Recurring rounds | Watcher, training and "no other duties" attestation, start and end, route, timestamped rounds (hourly per Whiting; per policy elsewhere), observations |
| Hot work permit | Shift-limited permit | Location, work type, permit-authorizing individual, operator, watcher, pre-work checklist, detector bypass link to impairment, watch end time, final inspection |
| Above-ceiling / penetration permit | Shift-limited permit | Location and grid, tiles opened, rated barrier ID and rating, per-penetration UL system #, installer certification, before-close photos, post-work checklist, facility close signature ([Northwell](https://www.northwell.edu/sites/northwell.edu/files/2024-01/daily-above-the-ceiling-work-permit.pdf)) |
| Training / orientation roster | Per worker | Worker, company, topics (ICRA, ILSM, exits, extinguishers, hot work), card or certificate and expiry, signature |
| Deficiency / rework | Event | Found by, time, photo, location, severity (stop-work flag), responsible party, corrective action, re-inspection, close signature; written notice to IP and EH&S ([UNC EHS0050](https://spice.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Safety-and-Infection-Control-Management-Plan-for-Construction-Renovation-and-Modernization-Projects-EHS0050.pdf)) |

ASHE publishes a combined "ILSM-ICRA precaution daily monitoring checklist" ([ASHE](https://www.ashe.org/ilsm-icra-precaution-daily-monitoring-checklist)). That suggests owners expect one daily walk covering both. Barriers are where the two overlap: a single barrier item should capture smoke-tightness (ILSM) and dust-tightness and pressure (ICRA), with one photo set satisfying both.

## Defensible evidence means append-only events, two clocks and hashed photos

No current Joint Commission or CMS statement on electronic signatures for EC/LS records was found. The legal floor is therefore ESIGN/UETA: intent to sign, consent (which can be implied between businesses), signature attached to the record, and accurate retention and reproduction ([Adobe ESIGN](https://www.adobe.com/sign/hub/how-to/what-is-the-e-sign-act.html); [Arvato/DocuSign whitepaper](https://us.arvato-systems.com/resource/blob/18248/1679f579e329326a73a3a4bc57df2bf1/arvato-systems-esigning-docusign-legal-whitepaper-ueta-esign-en-data.pdf)). UETA is law in 49 states. New York uses its own statute ([DocuSign](https://www.docusign.com/learn/electronic-signature-laws-state)). 21 CFR Part 11 does not apply to construction records, but it is the right design model. It calls for computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails in which changes "must not obscure earlier values," and signature manifests showing printed name, date and time, and meaning ([Ofni Systems](https://www.ofnisystems.com/21-cfr-11-10e-audit-trails/); [Klyverity](https://klyverity.com/blog/part-11-audit-trail-requirements)). EXIF metadata can be edited without leaving a trace. C2PA-style signing is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof ([C2PA FAQ](https://c2pa.org/faqs/); [arXiv 2604.24890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24890)). For a hospital, the larger photo risk is HIPAA. Faces, wristbands, whiteboards, and monitors in the background count as identifiers ([HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-photography-rules/)).

Your "any team member can uncheck (logged)" rule fits this model, provided an uncheck is a new event and never an edit. In concrete terms, the evidence layer needs: an append-only, hash-chained event log; a signature manifest showing name, role, meaning ("performed," "verified," "co-signed," "rework requested"), server UTC, and device time; a SHA-256 hash of each photo computed at capture, with the original kept alongside any annotated copy; device capture time and server receipt time, with large gaps flagged; a privacy prompt and blur tool at capture; and photos stored in the app sandbox, never the camera roll. Retention periods for ICRA and ILSM records are not set by any source found here. They probably come from hospital policy, contracts, and construction statutes of repose, and need a separate legal check.

## Incumbents sell to hospitals; nobody owns the contractor's daily walk

The purpose-built tools are hospital-facing. **Soleran ILSM Manager** sends assessments to the relevant parties for daily or weekly mobile inspections, with dashboards by accrediting standard. **INCUE** digitizes ICRA containment with sensors. **Oxmaint** and **Coast** are CMMS modules that cover ILSM, fire watch, and permits. **CRx**, **SafeQual**, and **Veoci** handle rounds and permits for facilities and IP buyers. **Ruya Compliance** attaches ICRA levels and permit photos to building records and has contractor portals. UC Davis tracks ICRAs in Smartsheet ([Soleran](https://www.soleran.com/applications/interim-life-safety-measure-manager); [INCUE](https://incue.app/); [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/healthcare/interim-life-safety-measures-hospital-construction); [Veoci](https://veoci.com/hospitals-healthcare/eoc-rounding); [Ruya](https://ruyacompliance.com/); [UC Davis ILSM](https://health.ucdavis.edu/fire/interim-life-safety-measures)). Contractor platforms are generic. **SafetyCulture** hosts a handful of user-contributed ICRA and ILSM templates at about **$24/user/month**. **Fieldwire** Pro runs **$39/user/month** and has a mature firestop workflow (photo, UL system dropdown, QR code per penetration). **Procore** costs roughly **$10k–$50k+/year**, priced on construction volume. **Autodesk Build** is about **$1,625–$1,740/user/year**. None ships ICRA class logic, ILSM clocks, or IP co-sign ([SafetyCulture library](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/mercy-muskegon-icra-checklist); [Fieldwire firestop](https://www.fieldwire.com/usecase/firestop-documentation/); [dailybuild](https://dailybuild.co/construction-software-pricing-guide); [Autodesk](https://getconstructioncloud.autodesk.com/buy-autodesk-build)). Field reviews point to the adoption risk you will face. In Capterra's aggregate, 66% of 145 negative Procore reviews cite slowness, and reviewers say "the biggest hurdle ... is getting subcontractors, vendors, and superintendents to buy-in" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/56250/Procore/reviews/)). Offline support is partial across incumbents ([ABLEMKR](https://ablemkr.com/offline-construction-app-what-works-without-signal/)). No public pricing, user reviews, or demo videos were found for any purpose-built ICRA/ILSM tool. The gap is real, but how well it can be defended is unproven.

The demand side is steady rather than booming. The US has **6,100 hospitals** ([AHA Fast Facts 2026](https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals-2017)), each with continual renovation churn, and healthcare construction runs roughly **$50–75B a year**. Census year-to-date 2026 is down, while FMI, AIA, and ConstructConnect forecast growth ([Census](https://www.census.gov/construction/c30/pdf/release.pdf); [HFMA](https://www.hfma.org/fast-finance/hospital-construction-spending-forecast-2030/)). There is a distribution channel in the workforce. The Carpenters union runs 8-, 24-, and 40-hour ICRA courses, and one regional council reports **3,467** members of other crafts through its 8-hour awareness course ([CMW Carpenters](https://www.cmwcarpenters.com/icra-program-continues-to-make-hospital-construction-safer/?lang=es); [CPWR](https://www.cpwr.com/training/training-programs/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra/)). A vendor-sponsored STARC/ASHE survey of 150+ respondents found about **67% had adopted ICRA 2.0**. It also named emergency work, where an ICRA meeting before work "isn't always practical," as a pain point ([PSQH](https://www.psqh.com/analysis/new-data-reveals-icra-2-0-implementation-challenges/)).

## Twelve adjustments to the proposal

The proposal's evidence model (photos, e-signature, timestamps, co-sign, uncheck logging, immutable trail, PDF export) matches what owners and surveyors expect. The structural assumptions need the changes below. They are inferences from the evidence above.

| # | Adjustment | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make permits first-class objects with a lifecycle**: draft → approved → active → re-assessed (new version) → downgraded → closeout gates → closed. Checklists hang off permits. | Compliance is judged per permit and zone; scope creep without a new sign-off is a recurring gap; surveyors want version history. |
| 2 | **Separate recurring logs from one-off checklists.** Daily ICRA, pressure, egress, housekeeping, and fire watch rounds are scheduled series with gap detection ("missed day" alerts). Setup and closeout are one-off. | CDC daily; Joint Commission daily exit checks; monthly temporary-system tests; a missing day reads as non-performance. |
| 3 | **Ship a configurable, versioned ICRA matrix and ILSM measure library per owner**, stamped on each permit, with a special-hazard modifier (sewage/mold) and an adjacent-area step. | ASHE, CHA, and UC Davis cells differ; the Joint Commission makes the hospital's policy authoritative for ILSM. |
| 4 | **Use Project > Phase > Zone/Containment > Permit > Log as the spine**, with floor, room, trade, and date as facets for filtering and grouping rather than as the hierarchy. | Zones span rooms and floors; risk crosses shared ceiling voids; large jobs run multiple ICRAs per phase. |
| 5 | **Hard-gate closeout** in the order above, with each gate signed by its owner (IP, EVS, engineering). The contractor cannot self-release. | Owner policies forbid reoccupancy on the contractor's declaration. |
| 6 | **Turn "uncheck" into a deficiency event**: it opens a rework item, notifies the IP and facilities in real time, and can raise a stop-work flag. | Communication breakdown is a primary cause of ICRA deficiencies; written notice of failures is required. |
| 7 | **Use structured pressure readings with per-permit thresholds**, plus CSV/PDF import from monitors. | Thresholds vary from 0.01 to 0.03+ in. w.c.; connected monitors already log. |
| 8 | **Add clocks**: a rolling 24-hour impairment timer (4 h / 10 h, overridable by state), hot-work fire watch countdown, and shift-expiring permits. | K-346/K-354; Missouri override; Northwell's one-shift permit. |
| 9 | **Require per-penetration photo + UL system # before above-ceiling permit close.** | Wall penetrations are among the most frequently scored Joint Commission elements. |
| 10 | **Give hospitals free reviewer seats, and test the hospital as buyer or mandator.** Pursue a Division 01 spec channel (01 35 33 infection control; 01 14 00.01 ILSM) where owners name the required documentation. | Owners hold every release gate; incumbents sell to owners; contracts already carry ICRA specs ([NY OGS spec](https://online2.ogs.ny.gov/dnc/masterspec24/docs/Division01GeneralRequirements/011400.01InterimLifeSafetyMeasuresMultiContract.docx)). |
| 11 | **Add an emergency/after-hours quick-ICRA flow and signature delegation** (who may sign for the IP at night). | Emergency work is a named ICRA 2.0 pain point; healthcare work is often at night and on weekends. |
| 12 | **Store regulatory references as a mapping layer** (legacy EC.02.06.05 / LS.01.02.01 → PE.03.02.01 and so on) and a worker training gate (card and expiry at permit sign-in). | Numbering changed in 2026 and policies still cite the old numbers; owners require training proof. |

For the 10–14 week MVP, narrow the scope to the combined daily walk. That means the ICRA permit (versioned), daily ICRA and pressure logs, the daily ILSM/egress log, deficiency and rework with co-sign, the closeout gates, and a per-zone PDF "survey pack." Move hot work, impairment clocks with fire watch, above-ceiling and firestop, and monitor integrations to beta and expansion. Voice notes and chain-of-custody fields can wait until interviews show a use: no source here describes chain of custody for construction ICRA or ILSM evidence. Offline-first capture with a local queue is a must for the MVP, not a phase-two feature, because hospital basements and shielded suites are dead zones and partial offline support is a known incumbent weakness. Test offline photo capture and signature in the PWA on iOS early in Discovery. On pricing, a per-project fee or a seat price at or below SafetyCulture and Fieldwire Pro ($24–$39/user/month) positions the product as an add-on that exports into Procore rather than replacing it.

## Conclusion

The field record that matters is the zone-level daily walk: barrier, pressure, egress, housekeeping, and penetrations. It is countersigned by people who work for the hospital, not the contractor. That changes the product from a better checklist into a **two-sided compliance ledger**. The contractor creates the evidence, the owner holds the gates, and the surveyor consumes the export. The contractor's motive to buy has grown more defensive (tamper-evident proof that controls were in place when an outbreak or lawsuit arrives). The owner's motive is visibility and control. A design that serves both, by putting configurable owner rule packs and free reviewer seats on top of contractor-paid field capture, gets around the main weakness of the hospital-facing incumbents without competing with them directly.

The second takeaway concerns sequencing. Because thresholds, matrices, and standard numbers vary by owner and just changed under Accreditation 360, the product's lasting value is the **rule-configuration layer and the immutable evidence layer**, not any encoded regulation. Discovery should test buyer and acceptance (will an IP sign in your app?) before feature depth. A second network-enabled research pass should read the ASHE, Joint Commission PE, and NFPA primary texts, so the first template pack is built from verified source documents rather than search excerpts.
