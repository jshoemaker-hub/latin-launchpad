## 1. Current ICRA framework: FGI, ASHE ICRA 2.0, CDC 2003

### Takeaway
ICRA is anchored in the FGI Guidelines (a design/construction code made mandatory where a state or AHJ adopts it) as one component of the Safety Risk Assessment; ASHE's ICRA 2.0 (2022) is the de facto field tool (Activity Types A–D x four Patient Risk Groups -> Precaution Classes I–V, plus a permit); CDC's 2003 guideline supplies the evidence-graded recommendations (negative pressure, barrier monitoring, daily documentation) that both FGI and accreditors point to. ICRA 2.0 is a best-practice tool, not a regulation, unless a hospital's policy or contract adopts it.

### Cited Findings
**FGI Guidelines (2022 edition, and the 2026 Facility Code)**
- FGI 2022 Hospital Guidelines table of contents lists Section 1.2-4.2 "Infection Control Risk Assessment" within the Safety Risk Assessment (SRA) sections — [FGI 2022 Hospital TOC](https://fgiguidelines.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/FGI-2022-H-TOC.pdf)
- The 2022 SRA lists ICRA as one SRA component alongside patient handling, fall prevention, medication safety, security, and disaster preparedness (1.2-4 Safety Risk Assessment Components) — [fgiguidelines.org](https://fgiguidelines.org/?p=12625) (search excerpt)
- FGI language (2018 ed., 1.2-4.2.1.1, as quoted in a carpenters' training deck): "An infection control risk assessment shall be part of integrated facility planning, design, construction, and commissioning activities" (quoted) — [Carpenters International Training Fund ICRA deck](https://www.mwhcec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Technician-Training-ICRA-Best-Practices-in-Health-Care-Construction.pdf)
- ICRA is conducted during early planning, before construction begins, and continues throughout construction; performed by a multidisciplinary team with expertise in infection prevention, direct patient care, facility design, construction, and HVAC/plumbing; outputs are infection control risk mitigation recommendations (ICRMRs) — written plans describing the specific methods by which transmission of air- and waterborne biological agents is controlled, incorporated into project documents and contractor requirements — [CASRAI ICRA guide](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction); [Carpenters ITF deck](https://www.mwhcec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Technician-Training-ICRA-Best-Practices-in-Health-Care-Construction.pdf) (secondary summaries of FGI)
- FGI states the ICRA is essential "to assure the new or renovated space will support the organization's infection prevention practices" — [fgiguidelines.org](https://fgiguidelines.org/?p=10696) (search excerpt)
- The first formal ICRA requirement appeared in the 1996 FGI (then AIA) Guidelines — [KDHE/KFMC ICRA presentation, Sept 2024](https://kfmc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/202409-ICRA-presentation-KDHE-HAI-AR.pdf)
- **2026 edition:** FGI released the 2026 "FGI Codes for Planning and Design" plus new handbooks dated Sept 1, 2026, on the FGI OneSource platform; the Guidelines were renamed "FGI Facility Code" (e.g., "FGI Facility Code for Hospitals"); the codes now contain only minimum planning/design requirements, with appendix-type guidance moved to separate handbooks — [Healthcare Design Magazine](https://healthcaredesignmagazine.com/?p=165444); [FGI podcast s2e1, 2026 drafts](https://fgiguidelines.org/podcast/s2e1-whats-new-in-the-2026-fgi-facility-code-drafts)
- 2026 changes reported include new water-age requirements / elimination of dead-end piping (relevant to waterborne-pathogen ICRA) and a new "Clinical Risk Assessment (CRA)" described as a parallel to ICRA for classifying exam/procedure/operating rooms — [Design Collaborative conference recap](https://designcollaborative.com/updates-from-the-healthcare-design-conference/) (secondary; drafting-stage); [Healthcare Design Magazine](https://healthcaredesignmagazine.com/?p=165444)

**ASHE ICRA 2.0 (2022)**
- In July 2020 ASHE convened a multidisciplinary team to revise the ICRA; key improvement was expanded descriptive language throughout all tables — [HFM, Oct 2021 p.42–43](https://hfmmagazine.mydigitalpublication.com/october-2021/page-43); [HFM: ASHE publishes revised ICRA guide](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4334-ashe-publishes-revised-infection-control-risk-assessment-guide)
- ASHE toolkit offers two free tools: "Matrix of Precautions for Construction, Renovation and Operations" and "Infection Control Risk Assessment and Permit" — [ASHE ICRA 2.0 page](https://ashe.org/node/682351); form PDFs: [ICRA 2.0 Form 2022-05](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/05/ICRA-2.0-FORM-202205%20Final.pdf), [ICRA 2 recent edits 2022-06](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/06/ICRA-2-recent-edits.pdf), [ASHE ICRA 2 form Jan 2023](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2023/01/ASHE_ICRA_2_TM_form_0.pdf), [ASHE ICRA 2 permit Jan 2023](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2023/01/ASHE_ICRA_2_TM_permit.pdf)
- Structure: Construction Activity Types A–D x Patient Risk Groups (Low, Medium, High, Highest) -> Class of Precautions I–V. Type A = "Inspection and non-invasive activities" (e.g., removing a ceiling tile for visual inspection); Type B = small-scale, short-duration activities creating minimal dust/debris; Type D = major demolition and construction — [ASHE ICRA 2.0 tool + permit](https://ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/08/ICRA%202.0%20Tool%20%2BPermit.pdf); [TX DSHS 2022 ASHE ICRA 2.0 presentation](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/IDCU/health/Healthcare-Safety/Conferences/2022/051922-2IP-ICRA2.0-ASHE.pdf)
- Matrix cells as listed on the ASHE-hosted permit (Types A, B, C, D): Low: I, II, II, III; Medium: I, II, III, IV; High: I, III, IV, V; Highest: III, IV, V, V — [ASHE ICRA 2.0 tool + permit](https://ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/08/ICRA%202.0%20Tool%20%2BPermit.pdf) (search excerpt); a CHA sample matrix shows different cells (e.g., Low/Type D = III/IV; Highest/Type A = II) — [CHA ICRA Construction Matrix Sample](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/ICRA-Construction-Matrix-Sample.pdf) — **conflict; verify against the ASHE PDF.**
- Changes vs. original ICRA: routine maintenance and "refresh" work (e.g., flooring installation, controlled sanding) now included in Type descriptions; an official step to assess impact on surrounding areas (dust, ventilation, pressurization, plus noise, vibration, and effects on data, mechanical, and medical gas systems); Class I remains for inspection; Class II "now ONLY for maintenance, not construction or renovation"; ICRA 2.0 added Class V — [Starc Systems ICRA 2.0 blog](https://starcsystems.com/blog/icra-2-0-healthcare-renovations/); [Accu-Tech ICRA 2.0](https://www.accu-tech.com/accu-insider/icra-2-what-your-ict-network-designer-and-installer-need-to-know); [TST Construction](https://tstconstruction.group/whats-happening/what-icra-20-and-why-it-matters-healthcare-construction) (vendor summaries)
- Special-hazard rule: for work involving sewage or mold, the ASHE document requires "Class of Precautions IV for LOW and MEDIUM Risk Groups and Class of Precautions V for HIGH and HIGHEST Risk Groups" (quoted) — [ASHE ICRA 2 recent edits](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/06/ICRA-2-recent-edits.pdf); [TX DSHS presentation](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/IDCU/health/Healthcare-Safety/Conferences/2022/051922-2IP-ICRA2.0-ASHE.pdf)
- Risk group change (single vendor source): "In ICRA 2.0, ALL patient care areas have been moved to the High-Risk Category, and all invasive patient care areas to Highest Risk" — [Starc Systems](https://starcsystems.com/blog/icra-2-0-healthcare-renovations/) (unverified)
- Conflict on activity-type count: one vendor claims five project types; the ASHE form labels A–D; UC Davis' institutional form adds a Type E column — [TST Construction](https://tstconstruction.group/whats-happening/what-icra-20-and-why-it-matters-healthcare-construction); [UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)
- Classic 4-step process: (1) identify construction type, (2) identify the patient/staff risk group most affected, (3) determine class from the matrix, (4) assign controls, "documented in the ICRA Permit" (quoted) — [CHA/AOEH ICRA and Permit guidance 2018](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf)

**CDC/HICPAC 2003 Guidelines for Environmental Infection Control in Health-Care Facilities**
- Recommends infection-control staff involvement in all phases of demolition, construction, and renovation, including risk assessment of necessary barrier types and "daily monitoring and documenting of the presence of negative airflow within the construction zone or renovation area" — [CDC Environmental IC Guideline PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf); [CDC Air section](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/air.html)
- "Create and maintain negative air pressure in work zones adjacent to patient-care areas and ensure that required engineering controls are maintained" — Category IB (quoted) — [CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- "Monitor negative air flow inside rigid barriers" — Category IC (quoted) — [CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- "Monitor barriers and ensure the integrity of the construction barriers; repair gaps or breaks in barrier joints" (quoted) — [CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- "Ensure proper operation of the air-handling system in the affected area after erection of barriers and before the room or area is set to negative pressure" — Category IB (quoted) — [CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- CDC engineering table: pressure differential > −2.5 Pa (0.01 in. water gauge) for negative-pressure (AII) rooms, > +2.5 Pa for protective environment; AII ventilation ≥12 ACH for areas under renovation or new construction (6 ACH minimum for existing facilities) — [CDC Air section](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/air.html); [Restored CDC MMWR RR-5210](https://restoredcdc.org/www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5210a1.htm)

### Inferences
- The app should model ICRA as a lifecycle, not a single form: (a) design-phase ICRA/ICRMR (owner + IP + designer), (b) construction-phase ICRA permit with class assignment, (c) recurring field inspections, (d) closeout. FGI demands it "throughout project construction," CDC demands "daily" negative-airflow documentation.
- Because cell values differ between ASHE and institutional versions (CHA, UC Davis Type E), the matrix must be a configurable, versioned table per facility (store which matrix version/edition was used on each permit).
- The sewage/mold override rule should be a matrix modifier flag ("special hazard present: sewage / mold") that bumps class.
- CDC Category ratings (IA/IB/IC/II) are useful metadata to show crews which items are evidence-based vs. regulatory (IC = required by state/federal regulation).

## 2. Controls required per ICRA class (checklist items)

### Takeaway
Controls are cumulative by class: Class I = minimize dust, replace tiles immediately, clean on completion; Class II adds active dust suppression, sealing doors/vents, HVAC isolation, contained waste; Class III adds critical barriers (floor-to-deck) and permit/IP sign-off; Class IV adds HEPA negative air, continuous pressure monitoring, anteroom/personnel decontamination; Class V (ICRA 2.0) adds a mandatory anteroom and stricter controls for highest-risk areas or sewage/mold. Closeout is gated: IP inspection -> EVS terminal clean -> HVAC isolation removed -> barrier removal.

### Cited Findings
**Class I**
- "Execute work by methods to minimize raising dust from construction operations." Replace any ceiling tile displaced for inspection immediately. "Clean work area upon completion of task." (quoted) — [APIC-hosted ICRA Matrix](http://apic.org/Resource_/TinyMceFileManager/Education/EPI_Intensive/Resourse_Documents/ICRA_Matrix.pdf); [ASHE ICRA 2.0 Form](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/05/ICRA-2.0-FORM-202205%20Final.pdf)

**Class II**
- Active dust control e.g., "water mist work surfaces when cutting," "seal unused doors," "block and seal air vents," "isolate or remove HVAC in the work area"; "Contain construction waste before transport" (quoted fragments) — [APIC-hosted ICRA Matrix](http://apic.org/Resource_/TinyMceFileManager/Education/EPI_Intensive/Resourse_Documents/ICRA_Matrix.pdf); [CHA ICRA sample](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/ICRA-Sample-Assoc-in-Occ-Envirn-Health-2015.pdf)
- Removed ceiling tiles / covers "shall be stored in a clean container or bag" (quoted) — same sources

**Class III**
- Isolate HVAC "to prevent contamination of duct system"; "Install all critical barriers (i.e., sheetrock, plywood, plastic), to seal area from non-work area" (quoted) — [APIC-hosted ICRA Matrix](http://apic.org/Resource_/TinyMceFileManager/Education/EPI_Intensive/Resourse_Documents/ICRA_Matrix.pdf)
- "Class III and Class IV determinations require a documented infection control permit and sign-off before work can proceed" — [CASRAI ICRA guide](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction) (secondary)
- Johns Hopkins 2023 permit: "Class III (Type C), IV, and V precautions require inspection and documentation for downgraded ICRA precautions" (quoted) — [JHM Infection Control Construction Permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)

**Class IV**
- Requires permit and approval; physical isolation; negative pressure relative to the surrounding clean corridor; exhaust through 99.97% HEPA filtration; "Construct and complete critical barriers meeting NFPA 241 requirements… Barriers must extend to the ceiling or, if ceiling tile is removed, to the deck above"; penetrations through fire-rated barriers must be appropriately fire-rated — [Healthcare Facility Hub ICRA 2.0](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/); [HEPACART Class IV](https://www.hepacart.com/blog/class-iv-infection-control-solutions); [5D CCS Class IV vs V](https://5dccs.com/icra-class-iv-vs-class-v-bay-area-hospital-containment/) (vendor/consultant summaries)
- Class IV anteroom: personnel pass through the anteroom and are vacuumed with a HEPA vacuum, or wear coveralls removed on exit — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/); [JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)
- JHM permit Class IV: "Install device (e.g., magnehelic, manometer, or digital monitoring) on exterior of work containment to continually monitor" (quoted); HEPA exhaust directed outdoors, not into shared/recirculating HVAC — [JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)
- JHM Section 01 35 33: controls by class include HEPA-filtered ventilation maintaining negative pressure, sealing penetrations, isolating HVAC; Owner may monitor all phases of barrier erection/removal/alteration by smoke testing, Baulin tube, or negative pressure alarms — [JHM Section 01 35 33](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Procedures.pdf)

**Class V (ICRA 2.0)**
- Class V "requires the construction of an anteroom that is large enough for equipment staging, cart cleaning, and workers"; anteroom optional/best-practice at Class IV but mandatory at Class V — [5D CCS](https://5dccs.com/icra-class-iv-vs-class-v-bay-area-hospital-containment/); [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/) (vendor; verify against ASHE)
- Vendor claim (unverified): recirculating HEPA-filtered air back indoors during Class IV/V is prohibited under ICRA 2.0 unless IP reviews and approves — [5D CCS negative air](https://5dccs.com/negative-air-pressure-systems-icra-compliance/)

**Waste / transport / all classes**
- Across classes: containment waste transported in covered receptacles; barriers not removed until the completed area is inspected by facility safety and infection prevention and cleaned by EVS — [UVA ICRA Policy Guidelines 2022](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf); [Scripps ICRA permitting process](https://www.scripps.org/sparkle-assets/documents/infection-control-risk-assessment-and-permitting-process.pdf)

**Closeout sequence (Class III–V)**
- Sample permit text: "Coordinate an Environmental Services terminal clean at completion of the work. Remove isolation of HVAC system in areas where work was performed. Do not remove barriers from work area until completed project is inspected by the Infection Preventionist." (quoted) — [UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf); [JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)
- Variant sequence: terminal clean of surfaces and diffusers; run negative air after dust-generating work until contaminants are flushed; barriers stay up until after cleaning; restore HVAC only after IP/engineering release; check supply and return registers for dust — [Healthcare Facility Guide](https://healthcarefacilityguide.com/design/infection-control-summer-construction-icra-protocols/); [UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)
- UC Davis: construction areas "must be inspected by the designee on the containment requirements worksheet for discontinuation or downgrading of ICRA precautions" — [UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)

**Water**
- Traditional construction ICRA tools focused on airborne fungi (Aspergillus) and "have not expressly included" water hazard analysis, waterborne-pathogen controls, and validation testing; a Water Management for Construction (WMC) ICRA tool has been proposed — [PMC9149880](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9149880/)

## 3. Negative / differential pressure: values, monitoring method, frequency, data fields

### Takeaway
There is no single federal construction-zone pressure number. The baseline reference is CDC/ASHRAE's ≥0.01 in. w.c. (2.5 Pa) negative for AII rooms; hospital specs commonly specify −0.02 to −0.03 in. w.c. for containments. CDC calls for daily monitoring and documentation; ICRA 2.0/institutional permits call for a continuously reading device (magnehelic, manometer, or digital monitor) on the outside of Class IV/V containments, often with audible/visual alarm.

### Cited Findings
- CDC: negative differential > −2.5 Pa (0.01 in. w.g.) for negative-pressure rooms — [CDC Air](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/environmental-control/air.html)
- "CDC and ASHRAE guidelines recommend a negative pressure differential of at least 0.01 "w.g." while AII rooms are generally designed for a minimum of 0.05 "w.g. (12.5 Pa) to hold containment during HVAC fluctuations (quoted) — [Curry International TB Center AIIR manual, Dec 2023](https://www.currytbcenter.ucsf.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/IC_2023_AIIR_December_2023.pdf)
- CDC: "daily monitoring and documenting of the presence of negative airflow within the construction zone" — [CDC guideline](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- Institutional forms require documented negative pressure at stated differentials such as −0.02 in. w.g.; a −0.03 in. w.c. (−7.5 Pa) minimum is commonly attributed to FGI/ASHE but was not verified against source text — [search synthesis of UC/medical center forms]; [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/) (unverified attribution)
- Class IV: continuous active monitoring; one vendor specifies digital recording manometer with visible display and visual + audible alarm below threshold; Class V described as 24/7 automated logging with alerts (vendor claim, not confirmed in ASHE text) — [5D CCS](https://5dccs.com/negative-air-pressure-systems-icra-compliance/); [HEPACART](https://www.hepacart.com/blog/class-iv-infection-control-solutions)
- JHM: monitoring device on exterior of containment "to continually monitor" — [JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf)
- A Section 01 35 33 spec template defines containment such that "Full containment always requires that negative pressure be maintained inside the containment area" and includes a differential pressure switch/gauge requirement (range not visible in excerpt) — [Contentstack-hosted 01 35 33 spec](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt3055f692fe7bf193/bltbfa218bdc2edb7de/66d8655c7297ea786867b1c3/013533-USER-INFECTION-CONTROL-PROCEDURES.pdf); [MWHCEC 01 35 33](https://mwhcec.org/documents/013533-FL-INFECTION-CONTROL-PROCEDURES-ABLE.pdf)
- Barrier integrity verification methods: smoke test, Baulin tube, negative pressure alarms — [JHM Section 01 35 33](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Procedures.pdf)
- Construction elsewhere can disturb pressure balance in a nearby AIIR or other sensitive room; pressure alarms can flag breached or poorly built barriers — [Healthcare Facility Guide](https://healthcarefacilityguide.com/design/infection-control-summer-construction-icra-protocols/)
- Industry practice: document differential at least daily while in use; logs with times and readings — [Envigilance negative pressure guide](https://envigilance.com/compliance/negative-pressure-room/) (non-authoritative)

### Inferences
- App data fields for a pressure reading: project/permit ID, containment ID/location, device ID + calibration date, reading value + units (in. w.c./Pa, sign convention: negative = into containment), required threshold for this permit, pass/fail (auto-computed), method (continuous display read / data-logger download / tissue/smoke visual), timestamp (auto), reader name + signature, photo of gauge display, alarm status, corrective action + time restored if fail, supervisor co-sign. Allow import of data-logger CSV as attachment.
- Frequency default: at least once per shift/daily (CDC "daily") for Class III–V negative containment, plus on any alarm, after barrier modification, after HVAC changes, and at start of work after weekend/holiday — configurable per facility.
- Threshold must be configurable per permit because values differ (0.01 / 0.02 / 0.03 in. w.c.).

## 4. Roles, permits, and sign-offs

### Takeaway
The owner (hospital) owns the ICRA: a multidisciplinary team (IP, facilities, safety, clinical, design/construction) assigns the class and mitigation; the IP signs the permit for Class III+ and must inspect before downgrade or barrier removal; facilities isolates/restores HVAC; the GC/sub executes controls and keeps logs; EVS performs terminal clean. The permit is posted at the work area.

### Cited Findings
- ICRA team composition: IP, direct patient care, facility design, construction, HVAC/plumbing expertise (FGI summary) — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- Class III and IV require a documented infection control permit and sign-off before work proceeds; facilities/construction sign-off alone is insufficient for higher classes — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- Downgrade or discontinuation of Class III–V precautions requires inspection and documentation by the designated person on the containment worksheet — [JHM permit 2023](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Construction%20Permit-2023.pdf); [UC Davis ICRA form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)
- Barrier removal only after IP (and facility safety) inspection and EVS cleaning — [UVA ICRA Policy 2022](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf); [UC Davis form](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)
- Facility policies apply ICRA to any work "that potentially impacts patient care activities, including work vertically or horizontally adjacent to patient care areas" — [UVA ICRA Policy 2022](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)
- ASHE provides a separate ICRA permit form (Jan 2023 version) intended to accompany the matrix — [ASHE ICRA 2 permit](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2023/01/ASHE_ICRA_2_TM_permit.pdf)
- Contract best practice: write negative air machine specs, HEPA 99.97% efficiency, and barrier standards into the contract (e.g., Division 01 Section 01 35 33) — [JHM 01 35 33](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Infection%20Control%20Procedures.pdf); [MWHCEC 01 35 33](https://mwhcec.org/documents/013533-FL-INFECTION-CONTROL-PROCEDURES-ABLE.pdf)
- Joint Commission (legacy EC.02.06.05 FAQ): staff and contractors need working knowledge of protective measures; the organization monitors the project and adjusts for unforeseen conditions — [TJC PCRA FAQ](https://www.jointcommission.org/standards/standard-faqs/hospital-and-hospital-clinics/environment-of-care-ec/000001278/)
- Contractor training: Carpenters International Training Fund offers ICRA best-practice technician training (a common contractor credential) — [Carpenters ITF deck](https://www.mwhcec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Technician-Training-ICRA-Best-Practices-in-Health-Care-Construction.pdf)

### Inferences
- Permit data fields (synthesized from ASHE/JHM/UC Davis form structure; confirm against actual PDFs): permit #, project name/number, location (building/floor/room), start/end dates, work description, activity type, patient risk group(s) of work area and adjacent areas, class, special hazards (sewage/mold/water), required controls checklist, HVAC isolation plan, negative pressure target, contractor company + superintendent + 24-hr contact, IP name/signature/date, Facilities signature, Safety signature, permit expiration/renewal, scope-change reassessment record, downgrade record, closeout signatures.
- Role-based signing: crew (performs + photo), GC super (daily co-sign), IP (permit approval, spot inspections, closeout), Facilities (HVAC isolate/restore), EVS (terminal clean attestation). Each signature needs name, role, timestamp, and device/geo metadata for audit trail.
- Pre-construction meeting record (attendees, ICRMR reviewed, training acknowledgment per worker) is a natural "project kickoff" checklist.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved mandating a formal pre-construction meeting or specific inspection frequency by IP (daily vs. weekly); these are institutional policy choices.

## 5. Joint Commission and CMS requirements and surveyor evidence (including 2026 changes)

### Takeaway
CMS §482.42 (mandatory for Medicare hospitals) requires the IPC program to monitor construction/renovation; surveyors ask whether an ICRA is done before work. Joint Commission (accreditation, deemed status) historically required a pre-construction risk assessment under EC.02.06.05 covering air quality, infection control, utilities, noise, vibration; on Jan 1, 2026 Accreditation 360 merged EC and LS into a new Physical Environment (PE) chapter with far fewer EPs, and TJC says substance is unchanged — the exact new PE citation for PCRA was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
**CMS (mandatory CoP)**
- CMS hospital infection control surveyor worksheet asks whether the hospital has policies for construction, renovation, maintenance, demolition, and repair, including an ICRA conducted before work starts; "No" cites 42 CFR 482.42(a), Tag A-748 — [CMS Hospital Infection Control Worksheet (S&C 15-12 attachment)](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/provider-enrollment-and-certification/surveycertificationgeninfo/downloads/survey-and-cert-letter-15-12-attachment-1.pdf)
- Interpretive guidelines: "The infection prevention and control program must include appropriate monitoring of housekeeping, maintenance, (including repair, renovation and construction activities), and other activities" (quoted) — [CMS SOM Appendix A](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/som107ap_a_hospitals.pdf) (via search excerpt)
- Tag numbering conflict: a third-party site lists §482.42 as A-0747 citing Appendix A Rev. 238 (March 2026), vs. A-748 in the 2015 worksheet — [Healthcare Pest Reference A-0747](https://healthcarepestreference.org/deficiencies/a-0747-infection-prevention-and-control/); [CMS worksheet](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/provider-enrollment-and-certification/surveycertificationgeninfo/downloads/survey-and-cert-letter-15-12-attachment-1.pdf)
- 2025 CMS citation data (TJC/DNV-accredited hospitals): "Infection control surveillance and prevention" ranked 13th with 62 citations (1.2%) — [Becker's 15 most common CMS citations 2025](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/15-most-common-cms-citations-in-2025/)

**Joint Commission (accreditation)**
- Legacy EC.02.06.05 FAQ: organization must have a PCRA process "in place, ready to be applied at any time if planned or unplanned demolition, construction or renovation occurs"; assesses risks to patients, staff, visitors or assets "for air quality, infection control, utility requirements, noise, vibration and any other hazards applicable to the work"; TJC doesn't prescribe a method, pointing to FGI and CDC; many organizations use a matrix linking construction intensity/location to protective practices; organizations need a process for minor work in low-risk situations using predetermined protective practices — [TJC PCRA FAQ (hospital)](https://www.jointcommission.org/standards/standard-faqs/hospital-and-hospital-clinics/environment-of-care-ec/000001278/); [TJC FAQ (home care version)](https://www.jointcommission.org/standards/standard-faqs/home-care/environment-of-care-ec/000001278)
- Critical access hospitals: revision consolidated into a single EP requiring a PCRA "when planning for demolition, construction, renovation, or general maintenance" that is then mitigated, cross-referenced to LS.01.02.01 (ILSM overlap) — [HFM: TJC revises CAH standards](https://hfmmagazine.com/node/699881)
- Accreditation 360: effective Jan 1, 2026; EC and LS consolidated into "Physical Environment" (PE); 464 EC/LS EPs consolidated into 63 under PE and the National Performance Goals (NPG) chapters; TJC states no new concepts were introduced and core substance remains — [Accreditation 360 FAQs](https://www.jointcommission.org/en-us/accreditation/accreditation-360/faqs); [TJC Prepublication Requirements, Jan 30, 2026](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/b56114d01df14bf18e39734a332d457d?v=653402a8); [McDermott: TJC 2026 updates](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/joint-commission-announces-key-accreditation-updates-for-2026/)
- The LS/EC rewrite was announced June 30, 2025, reducing hospital EPs by ~48% — [ASHE: TJC standards receive significant updates](https://www.ashe.org/joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates)
- Prepublication shows PE.04.01.01 covering building safety/facility management; the exact PE standard/EP carrying the PCRA was not confirmed — [TJC Prepublication Jan 2026](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/b56114d01df14bf18e39734a332d457d?v=653402a8); one secondary source states ICRA is mandated under the PE chapter for construction and major renovation — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)
- New IC chapter numbering cited by a vendor: IC.04.01.01 (infection preventionist responsibilities), IC.05.01.01 (governing body responsibility) — [Aerobiotix A360 IC](https://aerobiotix.com/accreditation-360-infection-control-standards/) (secondary)
- IPC findings remain among TJC's most frequently cited; in 2023 over 77% of surveyed hospitals had at least one IPC RFI — [TJC digital asset](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/99a8179a46c94470bfc95de256da3a29)
- A TJC/ASHE "Focus on Compliance" effort identified the most-cited physical environment standards, with inappropriate room pressurization among top issues — [ASHE/TJC (via search)](https://www.ashe.org/joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates)

### Inferences
- Evidence a surveyor will ask for (from CMS worksheet + TJC FAQ): written ICRA policy; ICRA/PCRA completed before work start (dated, signed); IP involvement; permit at site; evidence of ongoing monitoring (inspection logs, pressure logs); staff/contractor knowledge (training records; surveyors interview workers); adjustments when conditions changed. The app's PDF export should bundle exactly these by project.
- App should store standard references as editable metadata (legacy EC.02.06.05 -> 2026 PE.xx) because numbering just changed and many facility policies still cite old numbers.

### Gaps
- Exact 2026 PE standard/EP number for the PCRA and current CMS tag (A-0747 vs. other) not verified from primary documents (TJC and CMS hosts unreachable).
- DNV (NIAHO) and other AOs' construction ICRA requirements not researched.

## 7. Common ICRA deficiencies

### Takeaway
No published frequency dataset of ICRA-specific survey citations was found; consistent themes across facility and consultant sources are: ICRA treated as one-time paperwork, no reassessment on scope change, barrier gaps/degradation (top seals, doors), negative pressure not verified over time, missing IP sign-off, and impacts on adjacent pressure-sensitive rooms.

### Cited Findings
- Treating ICRA as a one-time paperwork step rather than ongoing monitoring obligation — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- "A Type B project that turns into Type C work without a new sign-off is a documented, recurring compliance gap" (quoted) — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- Barriers not sealed floor to deck, gaps at vents/pipes/ceiling grid; barrier integrity slipping over multi-week projects, especially floor-to-ceiling seals and high-traffic doors — [Reuzip ICRA containment](https://www.reuzip.com/blogs/news/healthcare-icra-construction-containment); [Higgins EDU](https://higginsedu.com/blog/what-is-icra-in-construction)
- Negative air installed but not verified throughout project — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- Missing IP sign-off for higher classes — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- Construction disturbing pressure in adjacent AIIR/sensitive rooms — [Healthcare Facility Guide](https://healthcarefacilityguide.com/design/infection-control-summer-construction-icra-protocols/)
- Room pressurization among top-cited TJC physical environment issues (Focus on Compliance) — [ASHE](https://www.ashe.org/joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates)
- IPC findings in >77% of TJC-surveyed hospitals in 2023 (all IPC, not construction-specific) — [TJC](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/99a8179a46c94470bfc95de256da3a29)

### Inferences
- Each deficiency maps to an app feature: scope-change prompt that forces re-classification + IP re-sign; recurring barrier-integrity checklist with required top-of-wall and door photos; pressure log with missed-reading alerts; permit cannot go "active" without IP signature; adjacent-room check items when AIIR/PE rooms are within the impact zone.

### Gaps
- No primary-source statistics (TJC SAFER data, CMS deficiency data) isolating ICRA/construction citations were retrievable.
- ILSM overlap (fire-rated barrier penetrations, NFPA 241 barriers, CAH PCRA cross-reference to LS.01.02.01) noted only; covered by another researcher.
