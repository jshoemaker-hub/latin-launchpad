## 1. What a typical healthcare GC's ICRA/ILSM program looks like: workflow, roles, sign-offs, order

### Takeaway
The workflow is consistent across owner policies and practitioner sources. The owner, not the GC, owns the ICRA. A multidisciplinary pre-construction meeting (IP, facilities/engineering, safety/EH&S, contractor) sets the ICRA class and ILSM needs. IP and facilities sign an ICRA permit before mobilization (required for ASHE Class III-Type C and all Class IV/V work). The contractor's superintendent or designated rep keeps daily barrier and pressure logs and fixes problems immediately. IP and the owner's rep do periodic rounds, usually weekly or more often. Barriers come down only after a construction clean, an IP inspection, an EVS terminal clean, a second IP/engineering inspection, and then release of HVAC isolation.

### Cited Findings
**Who owns the ICRA and the pre-construction phase**
- Completing the ICRA is "the responsibility of the health care organization requesting or contracting the work and/or in which the work is performed," and that organization must enforce and update precautions. Some projects need one ICRA; larger ones "can include multiple ICRAs for multiple phases" (date n/c) — [King Project Management, "Avoid These 10 Infection Control Pitfalls"](https://kingpm.net/infection-control-pitfalls-in-healthcare-construction/)
- Per a search summary, ICRA is a pre-project risk assessment that the IP team leads with facilities and construction staff, with barriers inspected daily. This is a Canadian vendor source, so treat it as practitioner guidance only — [InfectionShield](https://infectionshield.ca/ipac-during-construction/)
- A hospital policy describes a first screening step that decides whether a full Pre-Construction Risk Assessment (PCRA), ICRA and/or ILSM is needed. "If identified as necessary during the PCRA, an ICRA and/or ILSM must also be completed before work commences" — [NYU Langone construction process policy exhibit](https://nyulangone.org/files/exhibit-g2-nyuwuh-construction-process-policy.pdf); [CCE "Construction in Healthcare Facilities" (2022 upload)](https://cce-inc.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/19-Construction-in-Healthcare-Facilities-2.1.pdf)
- ASHE ICRA 2.0 crosses construction activity Type (A-D) with patient risk group (Low to Highest) to assign Class I-V precautions covering dust control, barriers, HVAC isolation and negative pressure — [Healthcare Facility Hub, "What Is ICRA in Healthcare? (ASHE 2.0)"](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/); ICRA 2.0 keeps 4 activity types and 5 precaution classes — [StarC Systems blog](https://starcsystems.com/blog/what-is-icra-2-0/); ASHE added the fifth class in its revision — [HFM, "ASHE publishes revised ICRA guide"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4334-ashe-publishes-revised-infection-control-risk-assessment-guide)
- Under ASHE ICRA 2.0, a formal infection control permit and approval are required for Class III Type C work and for all Class IV and V work. Facility policy may require permits at lower classes. The ASHE ICRA 2.0 Permit form is dated May 2022 — [ASHE ICRA-2.0-Permit-202205.pdf](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2022/05/ICRA-2.0-Permit-202205.pdf) (summary via search)
- The permit should be a control plan, not just a signature page: specifications, barrier drawings and verification methods. Facility rounds should catch missing permits — search summary of [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- ICRA zones should appear on drawings as formal deliverables. A barrier on a floor plan must line up with HVAC isolation, access restrictions, electrical, fire protection and operational planning (date n/c) — [The AEC Associates, "Healthcare Construction Documentation, Occupied Facility Phasing"](https://theaecassociates.com/blog/healthcare-construction-documentation-occupied-facilities/)
- Contractor expectations are given in writing before work starts. Containment failures are reported in writing to EH&S and the IP as soon as possible — [UNC Health "Safety and Infection Control Management Plan for Construction, Renovation and Modernization Projects" (PolicyStat, posted 2020)](https://spice.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Safety-and-Infection-Control-Management-Plan-for-Construction-Renovation-and-Modernization-Projects-EHS0050.pdf)
- One owner policy requires contractors to show prior negative-pressure experience with project descriptions and photographs or containment schematics (prequalification evidence) — search summary of [UC Davis Health Construction Dust Infection Prevention Best Practice Standard](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557740/addendum-1-attachement-1.pdf)
- Crews generally need ICRA training, hospital orientation and owner approval before starting work — [iRecruit, "ICRA Training for Healthcare Construction"](https://www.irecruit.co/insights/icra-training-certification-healthcare-construction-teams). Superintendents and PMs are trained to an "advanced" level (about 8-16+ hours, ASHE ICRA 2.0 or similar). Foremen and assistant supers get "operational" training — same source. Carpenters' union programs include an 8-hour ICRA Awareness train-the-trainer and a 24-hour construction ICRA course — [UBC Carpenters ICRA program](https://www.carpentersunion.org/programs/icra/)
- One hospital's model program, started after an Aspergillus outbreak in its heart-transplant population, required contractors and maintenance staff to complete IC training. A sticker on the hospital ID badge shows the training date — [Kidd, Buttner & Kressel, AJIC 2007, "Construction: a model program for infection control compliance"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17577484/)

**During construction: daily contractor logs**
- UVA Health policy (Aug 2022): the contractor's representative (RP) keeps the daily monitoring log and takes immediate corrective action. The owner's representative reviews logs and inspects the work zone. The RP confirms negative pressure "at all nonsealed openings into the Work site" and documents it on the ICRA log. Target pressure is about -0.1 to -0.5 in. w.c. (per search summary) — [UVA FM ICRA Policy & Guidelines 202208](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf); [UVA ICRA of Construction](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/InfectionControlRiskAssessment.pdf)
- A typical daily containment checklist covers sealed barricades, negative pressure and ventilation controls, debris handling and cleaning routes, PPE, contractor IDs, traffic control and signage, and permits — [UMMS ICRA 2.0 Containment Daily Inspection Checklist (SafetyCulture library)](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/umms-icra-2-0-containment-daily-inspection-checklist-mifucjjk2ja4lbzs)
- The ICRA permit, daily checklists and HEPA filter logs must be on site at all times. One practitioner source says checklists are typically completed twice per shift and signed by the superintendent (low confidence; source attribution in the search summary was ambiguous) — search summary citing [CHA AOEH Infection Control Guidance Documents (2018)](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf) and related policies
- ASHE publishes an "ILSM-ICRA precaution daily monitoring checklist" for daily logging of ICRA precautions used as interim life safety measures — [ASHE](https://www.ashe.org/ilsm-icra-precaution-daily-monitoring-checklist)
- A hospital "plan of the day" template argues the daily report is the main record used when an ICRA breach is alleged or a surveyor asks questions (vendor) — [PlanOfDay healthcare POD template](https://planofday.com/templates/healthcare-pod-template)
- A contractor's superintendent job posting requires the "ability to interpret Infection Control Permit requirements and implement site-specific control mechanisms" and notes frequent night/weekend work — [Auld & White Constructors job posting (Greenhouse)](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/auldwhiteconstructors/jobs/5150582008)

**IP / owner rounds**
- IP rounds are typically weekly or more often and check barrier integrity, dust control and HVAC impacts. Higher-category projects add continuous or daily pressure monitoring with documentation — [Healthcare Facility Hub, "Healthcare Construction and Renovation: ICRA, ILSM..."](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)
- Some owner guidance says the IP "should conduct daily rounds to ensure all elements of the ICRA are being met" — search summary of [CHA AOEH guidance (2018)](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf) / [Nebraska Medicine ICAP ICRA](https://www.nebraskamed.com/sites/default/files/documents/For%20Providers/ICAP%20Basic-ICRA-with-Matrix.pdf). Frequency conflicts across sources (daily vs. weekly).
- Infection Control Today says it is "not the IP's responsibility to instruct a contractor on how to complete a task." The IP sets requirements and verifies; the contractor chooses means and methods. It also says IPs are often not involved early — [Infection Control Today, "From Blueprints to Ribbon Cutting"](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/from-blueprints-to-ribbon-cutting-infection-preventionists-role-in-construction); [ICT, "The IP's Role in Construction and Renovation: Before, During and After" (2018 per CICTI repost)](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/infection-preventionists-role-construction-and-renovation-during-and-after)

**Closeout: barrier teardown and terminal clean (order of sign-offs)**
- Barriers stay up until the owner's Safety and Infection Control departments inspect the finished project. The contractor's own declaration of completion should not trigger reoccupancy. "A series of EVS terminal cleans may be required prior to removing the containment, or removal may be phased between cleans" — search summary of [CHA AOEH guidance (2018)](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf) and [UC Davis Health ICRA](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557730/icra-rev.pdf)
- Sequence described by one source: detailed construction clean, IP inspection, EVS terminal clean, second IP inspection, barrier removal. HVAC isolation is restored only after IP and engineering release. Negative-air machines keep running until contaminants are removed and before critical barriers come down — [Healthcare Facility Guide, "Infection Control During Summer Construction"](https://healthcarefacilityguide.com/design/infection-control-summer-construction-icra-protocols/); [Healthcare Facility Hub (ASHE 2.0)](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- ASHE ICRA 2.0 (as summarized): for Class III (Type C only), IV and V, IP (or designee) plus engineering must inspect before critical barriers are downgraded or removed — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- The contractor vacuums and cleans all surfaces in the finished area before barriers come down — [UNC Health EHS0050 policy](https://spice.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Safety-and-Infection-Control-Management-Plan-for-Construction-Renovation-and-Modernization-Projects-EHS0050.pdf). Post-construction, the IP works with the contractor, EVS and facilities on cleaning and readiness. HVAC and water lines are tested and flushed before occupancy — [ICT Before/During/After](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/infection-preventionists-role-construction-and-renovation-during-and-after); [CRE Insight Journal](https://creinsightjournal.com/infection-control-and-prevention-during-construction/)

### Inferences
- **Sign-off chain** (typical, reconstructed from multiple sources):
  - **Pre-construction:** the owner's project manager/facilities starts the PCRA/ICRA; IP + facilities/engineering (+ safety/EH&S) set the class and sign the permit; the GC acknowledges and posts it.
  - **Daily:** the GC superintendent or ICRA rep signs logs.
  - **Weekly or ad hoc:** IP and owner rep sign round findings, and the GC signs corrective actions.
  - **Closeout:** the GC certifies the construction clean; IP inspects; EVS performs the terminal clean; IP + engineering release; barrier removal; HVAC restored; occupancy.
  - **App implication:** an app should model this as a state machine with role-gated signatures.
- The same contractor rep often owns both ICRA and ILSM daily logs. ASHE's combined ILSM-ICRA daily checklist suggests owners expect one daily walk that covers both.
- Practice differs from owner to owner on inspection frequency (daily vs. weekly IP rounds, once vs. twice per shift), pressure units and terminal-clean count. The app needs owner-configurable templates, not a single hard-coded standard.

### Gaps
- No full text of GC (Brasfield & Gorrie, McCarthy, DPR, Turner, Robins & Morton, Gilbane, Skanska) program descriptions surfaced. Searches returned only vendor and secondary blogs, so I have no direct GC-authored workflow.
- Could not verify the exact UVA pressure thresholds or the "twice per shift" claim (fetch blocked).
- No Reddit or candid practitioner threads were found by search.

## 2. How large projects are organized (phases, floors, zones, trades, concurrent ICRA zones) and how documentation scales

### Takeaway
There is little direct published evidence. Sources confirm that big projects run multiple ICRAs, one per phase or zone, each re-evaluated when conditions change. ICRA zones are expected on drawings and must line up with HVAC, fire protection and access plans. No case study detailing multi-zone documentation volume was found.

### Cited Findings
- "More detailed projects can include multiple ICRAs for multiple phases of work performed" — [King PM](https://kingpm.net/infection-control-pitfalls-in-healthcare-construction/)
- Under ICRA 2.0, the ICRA should be re-evaluated when physical constraints or other conditions around a project change — [HFM, "The revamped ICRA 2.0 is more than a matrix"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4594-the-revamped-icra-20-is-more-than-a-matrix). LifeSafetyWiki calls treating the risk assessment as static rather than "living" a common Joint Commission citation — [LifeSafetyWiki LSRA & ILSM](https://www.lifesafetywiki.com/wiki/lsra-ilsm)
- ICRA considerations include "temporary provisions or phasing for construction or modification of HVAC and water supply systems" — [CAHO ICRA presentation](https://www.caho.in/files/ICRA.pdf) (Indian accreditation body; framework only)
- ICRA zones on drawings must coordinate with HVAC isolation, access, electrical, fire protection and operations — [The AEC Associates](https://theaecassociates.com/blog/healthcare-construction-documentation-occupied-facilities/)
- ASHE recommends using the ICRA for work by in-house maintenance staff as well as contractors, which adds many small concurrent ICRAs (above-ceiling work, etc.) on top of the construction project — [HFM, "Infection control during construction"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/3867-infection-control-during-construction)
- Shared ceiling voids spread risk across zones. A prospective study found Aspergillus spores only in areas sharing a ceiling void with the renovation and on the floor directly below. This is a Japanese study (MDPI, ~2025-26), non-US — [MDPI Infect. Dis. Rep. 18(5):101](https://www.mdpi.com/2036-7449/18/5/101)
- Pre-scan coordination meeting "with the hospital's facilities and infection control leadership to walk every zone" (vendor, scan-to-BIM) — [bim-services.us](https://bim-services.us/scan-to-bim-healthcare-renovation/)

### Inferences
- Data model implication: Project > Phase > Zone (ICRA permit with class, barrier type, pressure requirement, adjacent risk groups, including floors above, below and sharing a ceiling void) > daily inspections, plus ILSM items tied to the same zone. The app should also handle many small, short-lived ICRAs (above-ceiling permits, maintenance) alongside long-running construction zones.
- Re-assessment triggers (phase change, barrier relocation, HVAC change) should create a new permit version, not overwrite the old one, so surveyors can see history.

### Gaps
- No published case study quantified the number of ICRA zones, logs per week, or documentation hours on a large US tower or renovation project.

## 3. Common failure modes and costs (outbreaks, survey citations, stop-work, rework)

### Takeaway
Construction is a leading documented cause of healthcare fungal outbreaks. One cited figure is 49% of healthcare invasive-fungal-infection outbreaks. Failures cluster around barrier integrity, loss of negative pressure, ceiling-void and HVAC pathways, poor interdepartmental communication, and treating the ICRA as a one-time form. IP or owner staff can stop work on the spot. Recent high-profile events with legal and financial fallout include Seattle Children's (2019, air handling; OR closures) and the Harlem Legionnaires' outbreak (2025), where lawsuits name construction firms including Skanska USA.

### Cited Findings
**Outbreak evidence**
- A review of four decades of construction-associated fungal outbreaks found Aspergillus the usual agent, mostly pulmonary, with hematologic-malignancy and other immunocompromised patients at highest risk (Kanamori et al., 2015) — [PubMed 25870328](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25870328)
- "49% of healthcare-associated invasive fungal infection outbreaks" are linked to construction or demolition. This figure is cited in a case report from Thailand (2021 COVID ICU aspergillosis cluster, 4 of 25 patients), not a US source. The report treats 2 or more IFIs within a few months as an outbreak — [Cambridge Core case report](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/product/D3735C440503F13B578DD0C72D7900F8/core-reader)
- CDC: renovation and construction can disturb Aspergillus-laden dust and release bursts of spores, linked to clusters in immunocompromised patients. Gaps around filter banks and debris near poorly maintained filters contributed to outbreaks — [CDC Guidelines for Environmental Infection Control in Health-Care Facilities](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/media/pdfs/guideline-environmental-h.pdf)
- Seoul hospital study (2019): airborne fungal spore levels and the incidence of invasive aspergillosis were significantly higher during heavy demolition and excavation phases — [Antimicrob Resist Infect Control, s13756-019-0543-1](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s13756-019-0543-1)
- Kidd et al. (AJIC 2007): the hospital's contractor-training program came after an Aspergillus outbreak among heart-transplant patients — [PubMed 17577484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17577484/)
- **Seattle Children's (2019):** 1 patient died and 5 were infected with Aspergillus. All main ORs closed. The hospital said mold recurred for about a year due to air-handling deficiencies. Fixes included replacing an air handler, new humidification and sealing duct leaks. This was a facility HVAC failure, not a construction ICRA failure, but it shows the cost profile of an air-quality event — [CBS News, July 3, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/seattle-childrens-hospital-mold-death-shutdown-most-operating-rooms-delaying-surgeries-2019-07-03/?ftag=CNM-00-10aab4i); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/1-patient-died-5-others-infected-mold-operating-room-seattle-n1026596)
- **Harlem Legionnaires' (summer 2025):** 108 confirmed cases and 5 deaths as of Aug 19, 2025, linked to 12 cooling towers on 10 buildings including Harlem Hospital. Lawsuits allege untreated stormwater collected in cooling towers at a construction site next to Harlem Hospital. Defendants include Rising Sun Construction LLC and Skanska USA Building. Wrongful-death notices of claim were also filed against NYC. Skanska said it cooperated with the NYC Health Department on inspection and disinfection. No settlement status found — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/construction-companies-blamed-outbreak-legionnaires-rcna226104); [ABC7 NY](https://abc7ny.com/post/nyc-legionnaires-outbreak-new-lawsuit-accuses-construction-companies-negligence-harlem-hospital/17596612/); [ABC7 NY (Skanska suits)](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-lawsuits-filed-nyc-skanska-construction-behalf-victims-harlem-legionnaires-outbreak/17870860/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/infection-control/new-york-legionnaires-outbreak-tied-to-cooling-towers-serving-hospital-other-buildings/)
- **Non-US comparator, Royal Prince Alfred, Sydney (Oct-Dec 2025):** 6 transplant patients infected and 2 died. Construction for an A$940M redevelopment was suspected but not proven. Briefing documents show the transplant ward bordered the site at three points, including a balcony patients could use, and reports say established procedure "was not followed" — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/what-is-aspergillus-the-fungus-behind-recent-hospital-deaths-277067); [Ulladulla Times/AAP, "Safety guidelines ignored before hospital fungus deaths"](https://www.ulladullatimes.com.au/story/9187195/safety-guidelines-ignored-before-hospital-fungus-deaths/)

**Field failure modes**
- Recurring gaps: barriers poorly built or not maintained (unsealed, degrading over long projects) and failure to confirm negative pressure throughout the job — [CASRAI ICRA guide](https://casrai.org/guides/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction)
- A breakdown in communication among departments is a primary cause of ICRA deficiencies — [HFM, "Facility managers' role in infection control"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/2266-facility-managers-and-infection-control)
- Ceiling voids are a dissemination pathway — [MDPI 2025-26](https://www.mdpi.com/2036-7449/18/5/101)
- Stop-work: "infection control staff can shut you down on the spot," and a failed barrier can stop the project — summary of [Projul healthcare construction guide](https://projul.com/blog/construction-healthcare-hospital-building-guide/) / [Michuda, "Hidden Dangers of Healthcare Construction"](https://www.michuda.com/post/the-hidden-dangers-of-healthcare-construction). If a barrier is breached or negative pressure drops, work may stop until it is corrected — [CHA AOEH 2018](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf). Barrier failure means immediate work stoppage (Canadian source) — [InfectionShield](https://infectionshield.ca/ipac-during-construction/)
- Rework example: a missed utility dependency in an ED expansion forced a complete redesign of the phasing plan. The example appears illustrative, not sourced — [King PM](https://kingpm.net/infection-control-pitfalls-in-healthcare-construction/)

**Survey and regulatory consequences**
- Becker's analysis of CMS Form 2567s (2010 to mid-2026): A-0747 (IPC and antibiotic stewardship) produced 147 immediate-jeopardy citations, A-0749 (IC program) 110, and A-0700 (physical environment) 100. None is specific to construction — [Becker's, "10 CMS citations most often leading to immediate jeopardies"](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/patient-safety-outcomes/the-cms-citations-most-often-leading-to-immediate-jeopardies/)
- Oct 2026: Maine state surveyors said infection control issues at CMMC (Lewiston) could jeopardize its Medicare agreement. The construction link is not established — [Sun Journal, Oct 13, 2026](https://www.sunjournal.com/2026/10/13/state-says-infection-control-issues-at-cmmc-in-lewiston-could-jeopardize-medicare-agreement/)
- TJC historic most-cited lists are dominated by EC.02.06.01 and EC.02.05.01 (2014: 56% and 53%), with IC.02.02.01 at 70.9% in 2018. The construction-specific EC.02.06.05 and LS.01.02.01 do not appear in published top-10s — [Accreditation Quality Center 2014](https://www.accreditationqualitycenter.com/articles/ec-ls-dominate-2014-list-most-cited-joint-commission-standards); [PSQH](https://www.psqh.com/news/once-again-safety-issues-top-list-of-most-cited-tjc-standards/)
- CMS: survey-identified life safety deficiencies must be resolved within 60 days, with a Time-Limited Waiver for longer fixes. If a deficiency cannot be resolved the same day it is found, an ILSM assessment must be done — [Keyes Life Safety Compliance, "Corrective Maintenance Work"](https://www.keyeslifesafety.com/knowledge-hub/corrective-maintenance-work)
- Unverified vendor claims: "$15,000-$50,000 per violation" for Joint Commission findings, "paper-based ICRA records fail 34% of audits," and HAI outbreaks extending stays 4-6 days. None is sourced; do not use as fact — [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/pharmaceuticals/icra-checklist-healthcare-construction-renovation)

### Inferences
- The failure modes that matter most to a field app are the ones a daily walk can catch: barrier seal and degradation, pressure loss, ceiling-void openings, doors propped open, track-out, and dirty negative-air or HEPA units. The other big failure is communication: the IP and facilities not seeing contractor logs in time. Real-time flagging to the IP and facilities is the clearest value proposition.
- The financial and legal exposure for GCs is now concrete (the Harlem suits name Skanska). Contractors therefore have a defensive interest in timestamped, tamper-evident records proving controls were in place.

### Gaps
- No US peer-reviewed audit giving ICRA barrier non-compliance rates was retrievable. The Kidd 2007 and Moore & Huber 2014 outcome data were not visible in snippets.
- No US survey (2567) text was found citing construction dust or ICRA failure specifically, and no quantified stop-work cost or day counts.
- No 2020-2024 US construction-linked Aspergillus outbreak news surfaced in searches.

## 4. Deficiency flagging, correction (rework loops), and presenting evidence to TJC/CMS/state surveyors

### Takeaway
The expected loop is: find the problem on daily or IP rounds; correct it immediately or stop work; report containment failures in writing to the IP and EH&S; document the fix; and re-assess (ICRA re-evaluation, or an ILSM assessment if a life safety deficiency can't be fixed the same day). "Survey-ready" means the written ILSM plan and permits, logs proving measures were actually carried out (fire watch, drills, daily checks), workers who can describe the measures, and a living, current risk assessment, all available on site. Joint Commission's Accreditation 360 (effective Jan 1, 2026) folded EC/LS into a Physical Environment (PE) chapter, but ILSM requirements reportedly carried over largely unchanged apart from standard numbers.

### Cited Findings
- The contractor rep "take[s] immediate corrective action" on deficiencies found in daily monitoring, and the owner rep reviews logs — [UVA ICRA policy (2022)](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)
- Containment failures are reported in writing to EH&S and the IP as soon as possible — [UNC Health EHS0050](https://spice.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Safety-and-Infection-Control-Management-Plan-for-Construction-Renovation-and-Modernization-Projects-EHS0050.pdf)
- ILSM logging includes daily inspection logs, incident reports for barrier breaches or pressure loss, and training records, all subject to surveyor inspection — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)
- During active construction, surveyors typically ask for the written ILSM plan, fire-watch and drill logs proving the measures were done, and evidence that staff can describe them (tracer method) — [CASRAI ILSM guide](https://casrai.org/guides/interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm)
- A pre-survey readiness checklist lists ILSM documentation for any active project or deficiency — [Healthcare Facility Hub, "What life safety documentation should be ready before surveyors arrive?"](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/joint-commission-survey-documentation-readiness-checklist/)
- Keyes recommends separate EOC binders for each life safety feature that needs inspection or testing, ILSMs included — [Keyes, "EOC Documentation"](https://www.keyeslifesafety.com/knowledge-hub/eoc-documentation). A fire watch must be continuous and staffed by a designated person with no other duties — [Keyes, "MRI Trailer"](https://www.keyeslifesafety.com/knowledge-hub/mri-trailer)
- TJC changes effective Jan 1, 2023: LS.01.02.01 now requires hospitals to identify which ILSMs in their policy apply when a deficiency is found during survey, with the requirement moved from EP15 to EP1. One consultant noted that the policy must now explicitly include the assessment process — [HFM, "What you need to know about TJC's EC and LS updates"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4618-heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-joint-commissions-ec-and-ls-updates); [PSQH](https://www.psqh.com/news/tjc-tweaks-ec-and-ls-standards-adds-specifics-to-some-requirements/)
- Accreditation 360 (effective Jan 1, 2026) replaced the EC and LS chapters with a Physical Environment (PE) chapter plus National Performance Goals. ILSM requirements "remain unchanged, other than standard numbers" — [EH&E blog](https://eheinc.com/insights/blog/the-joint-commission-physical-environment-survey-process-2025-and-beyond/). Legacy standards: EC.02.06.05 (pre-construction risk assessment covering air quality, IC, utilities, noise, vibration) and LS.01.02.01 (protecting occupants during construction or when the LSC is not met) — [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/joint-commission). TJC also publishes an official "Hospital Life Safety & EC Document List and Review Tool" — [TJC](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/29635f929b954b6c93beed7ed2a7816b?v=8ca3c82b)

### Inferences
- "Survey-ready," as these sources describe it:
  - Every zone has a current, signed permit/ICRA with version history.
  - There is a continuous daily log with no gaps (a missing day reads as non-performance).
  - Each deficiency has a timestamp, photo, responsible party, corrective action and close-out signature.
  - ILSM measures carry evidence (fire watch hours, extra drills, signage).
  - Training records exist for each worker.
  - All of it can be pulled up on site within minutes during a tracer.
- Because standard numbers changed (EC/LS to PE in 2026), app templates should not hard-code TJC standard numbers in user-facing labels without a mapping layer.

### Gaps
- No source described exactly how GCs (as opposed to the hospital) present construction records to surveyors, for example whether the GC superintendent joins the tracer. Inferred practice is that the hospital's facilities/IP team presents, using contractor-supplied logs.
- New PE-chapter standard numbers were not confirmed.

## 5. Pain points with current documentation and stated wishes for digital tools

### Takeaway
Direct practitioner testimony was not found (no Reddit threads surfaced). Owner and consultant sources point to the same weak spots: paper binders (lost, damaged, misfiled), permits treated as signature pages, logs that must be on site at all times, and the need to keep one daily record defensible in breach investigations. Hospitals are moving to electronic ICRA templates (e.g., UMMS ICRA 2.0 electronic template with approval tracking, SafetyCulture checklist libraries).

### Cited Findings
- Handwritten logs in binders "can get lost, damaged, or misfiled." Digital storage lets results attach to the task they belong to — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/) (summary)
- The UMMS ICRA 2.0 electronic template is used for planning, assessing and permitting, and tracks approvals — search summary; see [UMMS checklist on SafetyCulture](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/umms-icra-2-0-containment-daily-inspection-checklist-mifucjjk2ja4lbzs). Hospitals (e.g., Mercy Muskegon) publish ICRA checklists in mobile inspection-app libraries — [SafetyCulture Mercy Muskegon ICRA Checklist](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/mercy-muskegon-icra-checklist); [Mitti LWH ICRA template](https://mitti.com/library/health-care/lwh-icra-assessment)
- The permit, daily checklists and HEPA logs must be physically on site at all times — [CHA AOEH 2018](https://cha.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/AOEH_Infection_Control_Guidance_Documents_2018.pdf)
- Keyes' binder-per-feature recommendation shows the binder-centric status quo — [Keyes EOC Documentation](https://www.keyeslifesafety.com/knowledge-hub/eoc-documentation)
- Missing or incomplete documentation can delay occupancy approvals or lead to corrective actions — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)

### Inferences
- Likely pain points (inferred, not directly sourced):
  - The same rep keeps duplicate ICRA and ILSM logs.
  - Pressure readings are transcribed by hand from manometers.
  - Photos sit on personal phones, disconnected from the log.
  - Sign-off authority is inconsistent (who may sign for the IP on nights or weekends).
  - Multiple owner-specific forms across a GC's portfolio.
  - Gaps in log continuity on night or weekend shifts, which are common in healthcare work per the job posting.
- A field-first app wins if it does three things: offline daily walk-through with photo-per-item, pressure entry with out-of-range auto-flag to the IP, and one-tap export of a zone's full history for surveyors.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, forum or quoted superintendent or IP testimony about documentation pain was found. This is the biggest evidence gap; primary interviews are recommended.
- No independent statistic on documentation failures (the 34% figure is unsourced vendor marketing).

## 6. Owner-side perspective: what hospital facilities and IP teams want from contractors

### Takeaway
Owners want:
- Early involvement of the IP (from concept, not after mobilization).
- Contractors who are trained and badged and show proof of prior negative-pressure work.
- Written acceptance of expectations before work starts.
- Daily self-policing with immediate correction.
- Prompt written notice of containment failures.
- Clean closeout that respects IP and EVS gates.

IPs set requirements but leave means and methods to the contractor.

### Cited Findings
- IP should be involved from concept and planning, but often is not — [ICT Before/During/After](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/infection-preventionists-role-construction-and-renovation-during-and-after); [ICT "From Blueprints to Ribbon Cutting"](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/from-blueprints-to-ribbon-cutting-infection-preventionists-role-in-construction)
- APIC describes the project team as IP, facilities, architect, engineer and contractor — [APIC Construction & Renovation 2019 addendum preview](https://apic.org/Resource_/store/books/preview/SLS9808_Preview.pdf); [APIC Text ch. 117](https://text.apic.org/toc/infection-prevention-for-support-services-and-the-care-environment/construction-and-renovation)
- HFM/ASHE: research links contractor training programs to better ICRA compliance (Kidd et al.). Moore & Huber (2014) found compliance improved after a construction-trained IP was assigned to construction activities — [HFM, ASHE revised ICRA guide](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4334-ashe-publishes-revised-infection-control-risk-assessment-guide)
- Facilities managers should make sure the ICRA process is properly carried out on every project, since interdepartmental communication breakdowns drive deficiencies — [HFM, "Facility managers' role in infection control"](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/2266-facility-managers-and-infection-control)
- Contractors are responsible for cleaning dust, debris, tools and equipment — [CRE Insight Journal](https://creinsightjournal.com/infection-control-and-prevention-during-construction/). The contractor vacuums and cleans before barrier removal — [UNC EHS0050](https://spice.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Safety-and-Infection-Control-Management-Plan-for-Construction-Renovation-and-Modernization-Projects-EHS0050.pdf)
- Prequalification: evidence of prior negative-pressure experience (project descriptions plus photos or containment schematics) — [UC Davis Health standard](https://health.ucdavis.edu/media-resources/facilities/documents/pdfs/9557740/addendum-1-attachement-1.pdf)
- Training-date badge stickers as visible proof of compliance — [Kidd et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17577484/)

### Inferences
- The owner-side "customer" for contractor documentation is the IP and the facilities director. They want visibility (live status per zone), proof (photos and pressure readings), and control (they hold the release gates). An app that gives owners a read-only or approve view of GC logs would match their stated desires, but buying decisions may sit with the owner, not the GC.
- Owner-specific prequalification and training-badge requirements suggest the app should include worker training verification (badge or QR scan at zone entry).

### Gaps
- No direct statements from named hospital facilities directors or IPs (e.g., HFM interviews) on contractor documentation quality could be read in full. Fetches were blocked, and search snippets did not include quotes.
