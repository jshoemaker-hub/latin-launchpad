## 1. Joint Commission ILSM standard (LS.01.02.01 through 2025; PE.03.02.01 from Jan 1 2026): policy, triggers, measures

### Takeaway
Through 2025 the ILSM requirement lived in LS.01.02.01 (EP 1 = written ILSM policy with criteria for when/to what extent EPs 2–15 apply; EPs 2–15 = the menu of measures). Under Accreditation 360 (effective Jan 1 2026) the requirement moved to the new Physical Environment chapter as PE.03.02.01, reportedly with substantively unchanged content; the measure menu (fire watch + fire-department notification at 4 h alarm / 10 h sprinkler, alternative-exit signage, daily exit inspection, temporary equivalent systems tested monthly, extra extinguishers, smoke-tight/noncombustible partitions, increased surveillance, housekeeping, extra drills, staff education/training) is what a checklist app must model.

### Cited Findings
- **[ACCRED] EP 1 – Written ILSM policy:** the hospital has a written ILSM policy that "includes criteria for evaluating when and to what extent the hospital implements LS.01.02.01, EPs 2–15 to compensate for increased life safety risk." — [Joint Commission EC News, June 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf); [JC Hospital LS & EC Document List and Review Tool](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/29635f929b954b6c93beed7ed2a7816b?v=8ca3c82b)
- **[ACCRED] Trigger scope:** ILSM applies when Life Safety Code requirements are not met (deficiency not immediately corrected) or during construction; a NY OMH policy reproduces the requirement under LS.01.02.01 as protecting occupants "when Life Safety Code requirements aren't met or during construction." — [NY OMH Policy 3036 ILSM](https://omh.ny.gov/omhweb/policymanual/3036-interim-life-safety-measures.pdf); [Duke ILSM operating instruction](https://www.safety.duke.edu/sites/default/files/IV_2InterimLifeSafety.pdf) (ILSM program "maintain[s] fire safety when code deficiencies can't be corrected right away")
- **[ACCRED] EP 2 – Fire watch + fire department notification:** fire watch is initiated when a fire alarm system is out of service more than 4 hours in a 24-hour period, or a sprinkler system is out of service more than 10 hours in a 24-hour period (JC EC News 2022). — [JC EC News, June 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf)
- **[ACCRED] EP 4 – Daily exit inspection:** "The organization inspects exits in affected areas on a daily basis." (the organization also determines when additional inspections are needed) — [JC EC News, June 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf)
- **[ACCRED] EP 7 – Temporary construction partitions:** "The organization uses temporary construction partitions that are smoke-tight or made of noncombustible or limited-combustible material that will not contribute to the development or spread of fire." — [JC EC News, June 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf)
- **[ACCRED] Temporary systems inspected/tested monthly** (cited as EP 12 in the search extract): "The organization inspects and tests temporary systems monthly." — [JC EC News, June 2022](https://store.jcrinc.com/assets/1/7/ECN_25_2022_06.pdf)
- **EP numbering has shifted between manual versions** (a 2008 document uses a different structure); confirm the current manual before relying on EP numbers. — [Hospital Life Safety Pre-Publication Version (2008-era)](http://mtpinnacle.com/pdfs/the-joint-comm-life-safety.pdf)
- **Measures surveyors look for (JC document review tool):** alternate exit signage, daily egress inspections, temporary equivalent systems during impairments, additional firefighting equipment, increased surveillance. — [JC Hospital LS & EC Document List and Review Tool](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/29635f929b954b6c93beed7ed2a7816b?v=8ca3c82b)
- **[BP] Hospital policy implementations of the same menu:**
  - Temporary but equivalent fire alarm/detection when a system is impaired, with monthly inspection/testing of temporary systems — [NYU Langone Policy 145 ILS Program](https://nyulangone.org/files/policy-145-ils-program.pdf); [Johns Hopkins Hospital ILSM](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20(ILSM).pdf)
  - NYU's first special measure: evacuate the building or notify the fire department and implement a fire watch — [NYU Langone Policy 145](https://nyulangone.org/files/policy-145-ils-program.pdf)
  - Additional fire-fighting equipment (JHH, Whiting), plus training on its use (Whiting) — [JHH ILSM](https://facilities.jhmi.edu/FacilitiesV4/DesignAndConstruction/HelpManual/Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20(ILSM).pdf); [Whiting Forensic Hospital ILSM form 2018](https://www.cga.ct.gov/ph/tfs/20190426_CVH%20Whiting%20Task%20Force/20190617_WFH%20Operational%20Procedure%20Manual/2018%20WFH%20Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20Form%20With%20Appendices.pdf)
  - Alternate-exit signage for everyone affected (Vanderbilt measure code "ILSM12"); Cleveland Clinic adds training on alternate egress routes — [VUMC ILSM Assessment Tool (12/2020)](https://www.vumc.org/facilities/sites/default/files/ILSM%20Assessment%20Tool-mh%2012-9-2020r1.pdf); [Cleveland Clinic ILSM SOP, Appendix M](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/files/org/supply-chain/non-employee-onboarding/appendix-m-interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm-standard-operating-procedure.pdf?la=en)
  - Extra fire drills: most common is one additional drill per shift per quarter (Vanderbilt: conditional on policy/safety officer discretion; UNMH: "one extra drill per shift per quarter, based on the outcomes of the assessment"); Whiting's 2018 form requires a minimum of two fire drills per shift per quarter — [VUMC](https://www.vumc.org/facilities/sites/default/files/ILSM%20Assessment%20Tool-mh%2012-9-2020r1.pdf); [UNM Health ILSM exhibit](https://unmhealth.org/about/bids-proposals/current-page-2019-and-before-proposals-pdfs/p377-17-exhibit-i.pdf); [Whiting 2018](https://www.cga.ct.gov/ph/tfs/20190426_CVH%20Whiting%20Task%20Force/20190617_WFH%20Operational%20Procedure%20Manual/2018%20WFH%20Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20Form%20With%20Appendices.pdf)
  - Smoking prohibited in the facility, "especially for contractors," and in areas adjacent to construction (only hospital source found addressing smoking) — [Whiting 2018](https://www.cga.ct.gov/ph/tfs/20190426_CVH%20Whiting%20Task%20Force/20190617_WFH%20Operational%20Procedure%20Manual/2018%20WFH%20Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20Form%20With%20Appendices.pdf)
  - ILSM implementation "to address a deficiency or during periods of construction shall be documented"; new risk assessments are needed when project scope changes — [Cleveland Clinic ILSM SOP](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/files/org/supply-chain/non-employee-onboarding/appendix-m-interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm-standard-operating-procedure.pdf?la=en)
- **Published ILSM checklist set** (LHA Trust Funds) exists as a template source — [LHA ILSM Checklists](https://lhatrustfunds.com/assets/uploads/documents/ISLM-Checklists-FINAL.pdf); TAHFM presentation "Managing the Interim Life Safety" — [Bartles, TAHFM](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.tahfm.org/resource/resmgr/imported/Bartles%20Bob.pdf)
- **Fire watch literature:** "Conducting the fire watch of standard LS.01.02.01" — [PubMed 19579471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19579471/)

### Inferences
- **Canonical ILSM measure list to model as checklist items** (wording synthesized from the sources above; frequencies from sources where given). For each item the app should capture: applies Y/N (from the ILSM assessment), responsible party, evidence type, frequency.

| # | Measure (checklist item) | Frequency / trigger | Evidence to capture | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| M2 | Notify fire department (and monitoring co.) + start fire watch | Alarm OOS >4 h/24 h; sprinkler OOS >10 h/24 h | Time of impairment start, notification time/person/agency, fire-watch start | Hospital (impairment coordinator); contractor often staffs watch |
| M3 | Post signage identifying alternative exits | At start of each egress change; verify daily | Photo of each sign + location tag | Contractor installs; hospital approves |
| M4 | Inspect exits in affected areas (incl. construction-area egress paths) | **Daily** (minimum) | Daily log entry per exit: clear/obstructed, signage, lighting, doors; photo on exception; inspector signature + timestamp | Contractor superintendent and/or hospital |
| M5 | Provide temporary but equivalent fire alarm/detection when system impaired | During impairment | Device locations, install date, test record | Hospital / fire alarm vendor |
| M6 | Inspect and test temporary systems | **Monthly** | Test record with date, tester, result | Hospital / vendor |
| M7 | Additional fire extinguishers / firefighting equipment (and training on use) | At setup; checked with daily/monthly rounds | Extinguisher ID, location, monthly inspection tag photo | Contractor |
| M8 | Temporary construction partitions smoke-tight or noncombustible/limited-combustible | At erection; inspect daily/periodically | Photo of barrier, material spec/data sheet, top-of-wall seal, penetrations sealed; inspector sign-off | Contractor builds; hospital inspects |

- The app's ILSM model should be **policy-driven, not hard-coded**: EP 1 makes the hospital's own ILSM policy the authority on which measures apply and when, so the app needs a configurable "measure library" per hospital/client with a per-project assessment that toggles measures on.
- Daily exit inspection (M4) and housekeeping (M10) are the highest-frequency recurring construction-crew tasks and are the natural "daily checklist" core of the app.

## 2. CMS requirements: 42 CFR 482.41, NFPA 101/99 (2012) adoption, K-tags, fire watch for impaired alarm/sprinkler, surveyor documentation expectations

### Takeaway
CMS makes NFPA 101 (2012) with TIAs 12-1 to 12-4 and NFPA 99 (2012) with TIAs 12-2 to 12-6 mandatory for hospitals under 42 CFR 482.41(b) (final rule May 2016, effective July 5, 2016). Surveyors use the CMS-2786 Fire Safety Survey Report K-tags; K-346 (fire alarm out of service >4 h in 24 h) and K-354 (sprinkler out of service >10 h in 24 h) require AHJ notification and evacuation or an approved fire watch — note the sprinkler threshold is 10 hours, not 4.

### Cited Findings
- **[REG] 42 CFR 482.41(b)** incorporates NFPA 101 (2012 ed., issued Aug 11 2011) with TIAs 12-1 through 12-4, and NFPA 99 (2012) with TIAs 12-2 (Aug 11 2011) through 12-6 (Mar 3 2014). — [42 CFR 482.41, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/482.41)
- **[REG] Effective date:** CMS final rule published May 2016, effective July 5, 2016. — [HHS guidance: Life Safety Code](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/life-safety-code); [ASHE 2012 LSC adoption resources](https://www.ashe.org/resources/alerts/2016/cms-lsc-adoption_2016-0503?page=67)
- **[REG] NFPA 99 exceptions:** facilities must comply with most of NFPA 99 (2012) except chapters 7, 8, 12 and 13 (per ASHE; not confirmed in regulation text by the search). — [ASHE 2012 LSC adoption resources](https://www.ashe.org/resources/alerts/2016/cms-lsc-adoption_2016-0503?page=67)
- **Citation conflict:** a CASRAI guide cites the NFPA 101 TIAs as 482.41(e)(1)(viii)–(xi) and the final rule as 81 FR 26872, while Cornell places them under (b) and cites 81 FR 26899. — [CASRAI NFPA 101 guide](https://casrai.org/guides/nfpa-101-life-safety-code) vs [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/482.41)
- **K-tags** are CMS data-tag row identifiers on the Fire Safety Survey Report forms (CMS-2786 series), not NFPA section numbers; when a K-tag is deficient, surveyors note the applicable LSC/NFPA citation on Form CMS-2567. — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/nfpa-101-life-safety-code); [CMS-2786U (07/2018)](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/downloads/CMS2786U.pdf); healthcare form [CMS-2786R](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/downloads/cms2786R.pdf) (copy also at [MO DHSS](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2024/10/CMS-2786R.pdf))
- **[REG/CODE] K-346 – fire alarm out of service:** where a required fire alarm system is out of service more than 4 hours in a 24-hour period, the AHJ must be notified and the building evacuated or an approved fire watch provided. — [CMS-2786W (2012 LSC)](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/Downloads/CMS2786w.pdf)
- **[REG/CODE] K-354 – sprinkler out of service:** same notification + evacuation-or-fire-watch requirement when out of service more than 10 hours in a 24-hour period. — [CMS-2786W](https://www.cms.gov/Medicare/CMS-Forms/CMS-Forms/Downloads/CMS2786w.pdf); an Indiana 2025 citation (facility failed to follow written policy when sprinkler OOS ≥10 h/24 h) — [IN DOH survey 2567](https://www.in.gov/health/reports/QAMIS/g0e421_2567.pdf)
- **Surveyor expectation – fire watch staffing:** watch personnel must be trained and "cannot have any other assigned duties during a fire watch" (state survey documents). — [MO DHSS CMS Fire Safety Information](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/archives/12905); [MO LSC Updates](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2018/09/Life-Safety-Code-Updates.pdf)
- **States can be stricter:** Missouri rule 19 CSR 30-85.022(11)(E) uses a 4-hour trigger for sprinkler impairment with immediate notice to the state department and local fire authority. — [MO DHSS CMS Fire Safety Information](https://ltc.health.mo.gov/archives/12905)
- **Surveyors expect a written policy** for impairments and that the facility follows it (Indiana deficiency cited "failed to follow written policy"). — [IN DOH survey 2567](https://www.in.gov/health/reports/QAMIS/g0e421_2567.pdf)
- **Example construction-area citation:** NJ surveyors cited 2012 LSC 18.2.8/19.2.8 (egress illumination) tied to new construction/renovation areas. — [NJ DOH survey report](https://healthapps.nj.gov/facilities/documents/PYHV11.pdf)
- **ASHE K-Tag crosswalk / survey readiness checklist** exists mapping K-tags to documentation. — [ASHE K-Tag Crosswalk (2021)](https://www.ashe.org/system/files/media/file/2021/02/ASHE-K-Tag-Crosswalk.pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Impairment clock logic for the app:** track cumulative out-of-service time per system in a rolling 24-hour window; alert at the threshold (alarm 4 h, sprinkler 10 h; allow a per-state/per-hospital override, e.g., Missouri 4 h for sprinkler). Many hospital ILSM policies start fire watch immediately rather than waiting for the threshold — make the threshold configurable.
- **Fire watch log fields implied by surveyor expectations:** watch person name, training attestation, "no other duties" attestation, start/end time, area/route, round timestamps (UNVERIFIED common practice: rounds every 15–30 min, per hospital policy), observations, AHJ notification record (agency, person, time), restoration time and restoration notification.
- Documentation a CMS surveyor would expect to see for construction (inferred from K-346/K-354 + written-policy citations): written impairment/ILSM policy, impairment log with start/stop times, AHJ notification evidence, fire watch logs, and evidence that egress/fire protection is maintained in occupied areas.
- UNVERIFIED background on other K-tags likely to be touched by construction (must be checked against CMS-2786R): K-100 (general requirements), K-211 (means of egress), K-321 (hazardous areas), K-345 (fire alarm testing/maintenance), K-353 (sprinkler ITM per NFPA 25), K-372 (smoke barrier construction/penetrations), K-711/K-712 (evacuation plan / fire drills), K-920+ (electrical/NFPA 99 items).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the CMS State Operations Manual Appendix I text or the exact K-tag for "construction, repair, and improvement operations" (NFPA 101 4.6.10 / 18.7.9 / 19.7.9). The CMS-2786R form lists these by section and should be checked.
- Did not find a CMS S&C/QSO memo specifically on construction ILSM; no evidence CMS requires "ILSM" by that name (it is a JC construct). CMS's requirement is LSC compliance + K-346/K-354 impairment handling + (for deficiencies) waivers/plan of correction.

## 3. NFPA 241 (construction/alteration/demolition), NFPA 51B (hot work), NFPA 101 construction provisions

### Takeaway
NFPA 101 (2012, CMS-adopted) lets a healthcare building stay occupied during construction only if required egress and fire protection features are in place and continuously maintained for the occupied portion (or AHJ-approved alternatives), and it points to NFPA 241. NFPA 241 (2022 is the latest edition confirmed) requires the owner to designate a fire prevention program manager who runs the written fire-prevention plan, hot-work permits and impairments; hot work follows NFPA 51B with a post-work fire watch (now at least 1 hour per 51B, vs. the older 30 minutes).

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE] NFPA 101 (2012) occupancy during construction:** buildings may be "occupied during construction, repair, alterations or additions only where required means of egress and required fire protection features are in place and continuously maintained for the portion occupied," or where the AHJ approves alternative measures. — [HFM/ASHE: Life safety considerations for construction projects](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4834-life-safety-considerations-for-construction-projects)
- **[CODE] Healthcare-specific:** healthcare occupancies must maintain egress and conduct daily inspections in work areas, referencing NFPA 241 (2009 edition, as referenced by the 2012 LSC); passive fire protection is required between occupied areas and the area under construction. — [HFM/ASHE](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4834-life-safety-considerations-for-construction-projects)
- **[CODE] NFPA 241 (2022) – Fire Prevention Program Manager:** the 2022 edition requires the owner to designate a Fire Prevention Program Manager whose role is to enforce the written fire-prevention plan, coordinate hot-work permits, and manage impairment of in-use fire-protection systems (vendor summaries; verify against standard). — [Engineering Fire Protection: Major updates to NFPA 241 2022](https://www.engineeringfireprotection.com/post/major-updates-to-nfpa-241-2022-edition); [LifeSafetyWiki NFPA 241](https://www.lifesafetywiki.com/wiki/nfpa-241); [US Made Supply NFPA 241 guide](https://usmadesupply.com/resources/guides/nfpa-241-construction-fire-safety)
- **[CODE] NFPA 241 2022 new content:** chapters on large wood-frame and tall mass-timber construction with their own fire watch rules, prohibition of torch-applied roofing for these types, and guard services in specific scenarios. — [Engineering Fire Protection](https://www.engineeringfireprotection.com/post/major-updates-to-nfpa-241-2022-edition); [Code Red Consultants: Updated Fire Watch Requirements](https://coderedconsultants.com/insights/updated-fire-watch-requirements/)
- **[CODE] Impairments under NFPA 241:** impairments must be managed and documented, with temporary fire protection or a fire watch covering the gap; an impairment fire watch is run by the impairment coordinator, distinct from a hot-work fire watch under the permit authorizing individual. — [Fast Fire Watch: NFPA 241 fire watch](https://fastfirewatchguards.com/nfpa-241-fire-watch-requirements-for-construction-sites/); [Redline Fire Watch](https://redlinefirewatch.com/blog/nfpa-241-fire-watch-requirements/)
- **[CODE] Hot work fire watch duration – sources conflict:** current NFPA 51B calls for a post-hot-work fire watch of at least 1 hour (with additional monitoring where conditions warrant) — [LifeSafetyWiki](https://www.lifesafetywiki.com/wiki/nfpa-241); another source lists 30 minutes after (longer per AHJ) — [US Made Supply fire watch guide](https://usmadesupply.com/resources/guides/fire-watch-requirements). Massachusetts 527 CMR 1.00 §16.19.1.4.2 requires a 2-hour watch after torch-applied roofing, and MA references the 2019 edition of NFPA 51B alongside NFPA 241 (2022). — [Fast Fire Watch](https://fastfirewatchguards.com/nfpa-241-fire-watch-requirements-for-construction-sites/)
- **AHJ checklists exist:** e.g., City of Beverly, MA "Fire Safety Checklist for Construction, Rehabilitation, and Demolition Sites" (NFPA 241). — [Beverly MA NFPA 241 checklist](https://www.beverlyma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6353/NFPA-241-Checklist)
- **Adoption varies by jurisdiction; local amendments and the AHJ can override baseline requirements.** — [Fast Fire Watch](https://fastfirewatchguards.com/nfpa-241-fire-watch-requirements-for-construction-sites/)

### Inferences
- **Hot work permit data fields** (inferred from NFPA 51B/241 structure; UNVERIFIED in detail): permit #, location, work type (welding/cutting/brazing/grinding/torch roofing), date/time valid (single shift), permit authorizing individual (PAI) signature, operator name, fire watch name, pre-work checklist (combustibles moved 35 ft or shielded, floors swept, openings covered, sprinklers/detection in service or impaired-with-ILSM, smoke detectors covered/bypassed with hospital approval, extinguisher present), fire watch end time (≥1 h post-work, per 51B; configurable to AHJ), final inspection signature. In hospitals, detector bypass for hot work is itself an impairment that ties back to the ILSM/impairment log.
- The **NFPA 241 Fire Prevention Program Manager** maps naturally to an app role ("FPPM") with authority to issue/close hot-work permits and impairments; on hospital projects the owner (hospital) designates this person, though contractor superintendents often perform day-to-day tasks.
- Field checklist items driven by NFPA 101/241 for occupied healthcare: daily egress inspection of construction-affected paths; verification that rated separations between work area and occupied area are intact; standpipes/fire department access maintained; temporary heating equipment listed and attended (UNVERIFIED detail); combustible waste removed daily.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether an NFPA 241 **2025** edition has been issued (searches surfaced only 2022 as current). Standard text not accessible; requirements on temporary heating, standpipes (e.g., standpipe within one floor of highest level), and site security were not verified.
- NFPA 51B current edition (2019 vs. a later edition) and its exact fire-watch/monitoring durations (1 h watch + possible additional monitoring) not verified from the standard.
- Exact text of NFPA 101 (2012) 4.6.10, 18.7.9 and 19.7.9 not retrieved.

## 4. Impairment management (NFPA 25), above-ceiling permits, penetrations/firestop, rated doors and smoke compartments

### Takeaway
NFPA 25 Chapter 15 requires an impairment coordinator who authorizes every preplanned impairment, tags the system (at FDCs and control valves), and notifies the AHJ/fire department, monitoring company, insurer and occupants at start and on restoration; compensating measures are required if a water-based system is out >10 h in 24 h. Hospitals extend barrier management through above-ceiling and barrier-penetration permits (often valid one shift) with pre- and post-work inspections and firestop attestation — directly tied to the most-cited Joint Commission findings.

### Cited Findings
- **[CODE] NFPA 25 §15.5 preplanned impairments:** "All preplanned impairments shall be authorized by the impairment coordinator"; before authorization the coordinator verifies the extent and expected duration have been determined. — [NFPA 25 Ch. 15 excerpt (NV Risk Mgmt)](https://risk.nv.gov/uploadedfiles/risknvgov/content/Loss_Prevention/Impairments.pdf)
- **[CODE] Compensating measures:** when a water-based fire protection system is out of service more than 10 hours in a 24-hour period, the impairment coordinator must arrange one or more compensating measures (e.g., fire watch) (secondary summary; not confirmed against official text). — [Zion: NFPA 25 §15.5.2 step by step](https://zion.us/fire-watch/nfpa-25-section-15-5-2-step-by-step/)
- **[CODE] Notifications:** AHJ, fire department, fire alarm/monitoring company, insurance carrier, property owner and occupants, and the coordinator's supervisor; on restoration the insurer, alarm company, owner and AHJs are notified that protection is restored. — [Kord Fire Protection](https://kordfire.com/nfpa-25-fire-protection-system-impairment-procedures/); [JF Ahern: Owner impairment responsibilities](https://www.jfahern.com/blog/2023/06/14/fire-protection-system-building-owner-impairment-responsibilities); [NFPA Today blog on restoration](https://www.nfpa.org/News-and-Research/Publications-and-media/Blogs-Landing-Page/NFPA-Today/Blog-Posts/2021/02/26/NFPA-25-provides-guidance-on-maximizing-fire-safety-during-sprinkler-systems-restoration-process)
- **[CODE] Tagging (§15.3):** an impairment tag is posted at each fire department connection and the system control valve, and other locations required by the AHJ. — [NFPA 25 Ch. 15 excerpt (NV)](https://risk.nv.gov/uploadedfiles/risknvgov/content/Loss_Prevention/Impairments.pdf)
- **Editions:** several sources quote NFPA 25 (2011), which is the edition referenced through the CMS-adopted 2012 LSC; one source notes the 2026 edition expanded impairment procedures. — [Kord Fire](https://kordfire.com/nfpa-25-fire-protection-system-impairment-procedures/); [Speedy Fire Protection: deficiency vs impairment](https://www.speedyfireprotection.com/firesprinkler-insights-and-updates/blog/fire-sprinkler-inspection-report-deficiency-impairment-nfpa-25)
- **Iowa State Fire Marshal** issued a memo on impairment/fire watch handling. — [Iowa SFM memo](https://dial.iowa.gov/media/7413/download?inline=)
- **[BP] Barrier penetration permit scope (NH Hospital):** a permit is required for "the creation of any through-penetrations in any ceiling, floor, wall, or building assembly" and for equipment installed above ceilings (overhead, plenum, interstitial space). — [NH Hospital Fire/Smoke Barrier Penetration Permit policy (RFP-2026 App. F)](https://www.dhhs.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt476/files/inline-documents/sonh/rfp-2026-nhh-04-fireb-app-f-appendix-f-fire-smoke-barrier-penetration-permit.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Impairment record data model:** system (sprinkler zone/riser, fire alarm zone, smoke detection, kitchen hood, standpipe, fire pump), planned vs. emergency, authorized by (impairment coordinator), start time, expected duration, tags posted (FDC/control valve photos), notifications (AHJ/FD, monitoring co., insurer, hospital operator/house supervisor) each with time + person, compensating measures (fire watch, temp detection, extra extinguishers) linked to ILSM measures, rolling-24h duration counter, restoration time, restoration test, restoration notifications, tag removal photo.
- **Above-ceiling permit data model:** permit #, contractor/company, worker names, date + shift validity, location (building/floor/room/grid), ceiling tiles to be removed, barriers to be penetrated (rated wall ID from life safety drawings, rating), scope, ICRA class link (overlap), pre-work inspection (photo + inspector), firestop system UL/listing number per penetration, installer certification, post-work inspection checklist (penetrations sealed, systems supported, tiles restored, no debris, sprinkler heads unobstructed), photos of each penetration before closing ceiling, facility sign-off to close permit.
- Penetration photos must be taken **before ceilings close** — the app should block permit close-out without per-penetration photos + listing number, because unsealed penetrations are among the most-cited JC findings (Section 7).
- Rated door / smoke compartment integrity checks (doors latch, closers work, no wedges, no holes in smoke barriers above ceilings) belong in the daily/shift construction-area round; they map to JC LS.02.01.10/LS.02.01.30 (pre-2026) and to K-tags for smoke barriers/doors (UNVERIFIED tag numbers).

## 6. Pre-Construction Risk Assessment (EC.02.06.05) and its 2026 status; Accreditation 360 changes

### Takeaway
Through 2025, EC.02.06.05 required a PCRA covering air quality, infection control, utilities, noise, vibration and other hazards before demolition/construction/renovation/maintenance, plus action to minimize those risks; ILSM is typically decided as part of or alongside the PCRA. On Jan 1 2026 Accreditation 360 merged the EC and LS chapters into a single Physical Environment (PE) chapter (~48% fewer EPs for hospitals), placing ILSM in PE.03.02.01; secondary sources say ILSM content is unchanged other than numbering. The new PE number for the PCRA requirement was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **[ACCRED] EC.02.06.05 PCRA text:** "When planning for demolition, construction, renovation, or general maintenance, the hospital conducts a preconstruction risk assessment for air quality requirements, infection control, utility requirements, noise, vibration, and other hazards that affect care, treatment, and services"; a companion EP: "The hospital takes action based on its assessment to minimize risks during demolition, construction, or renovation." The process must also cover unplanned work posing risk. — [HFM: Pre-construction risk assessment](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4674-preconstruction-risk-assessment-for-health-care-facilities); [EC.02.06.05 Managing Construction (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/714718097/EC-02-06-05-Managing-Construction-1)
- **The standard is not prescriptive; ICRA is usually described as the most important part of the PCRA.** — [HFM](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4674-preconstruction-risk-assessment-for-health-care-facilities); ASHE ICRA 2.0 context — [Vertex: ICRA 2.0](https://vertexeng.com/insights/best-practices-discussing-icra-2-0-in-healthcare/)
- **[ACCRED] Accreditation 360 effective Jan 1 2026:** EC and LS chapters combined into one Physical Environment chapter with selected standards in a new National Performance Goals chapter. — [HFM/ASHE Fall 2026: role of the physical environment](https://hfmmagazine.mydigitalpublication.com/publication/?i=870252&article_id=5212014&view=articleBrowser); [EH&E blog](https://eheinc.com/insights/blog/the-joint-commission-physical-environment-survey-process-2025-and-beyond/); [AHA news, June 30 2025](https://aha.org/news/headline/2025-06-30-joint-commission-streamlines-accreditation-process-removes-over-700-requirements)
- **Scale:** EC + LS previously had 44 standards / 396 EPs; EPs reduced 48% for hospitals and 46% for CAHs. — [ASHE: JC standards receive significant updates](https://www.ashe.org/joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates); [AHE](https://www.ahe.org/news/advocacy/2025-07-02-joint-commission-standards-receive-significant-updates)

- **ILSM = PE.03.02.01; Water Management = PE.04.01.05** (dedicated sections), per a 2026 TAHFM presentation by JC's George Mills. — [TAHFM 2026, Mills](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.tahfm.org/resource/resmgr/annual_conference/2026/doc_mills_george115tuesday.pdf); also [NEHES 2026, Marcoux](https://cdn.ymaws.com/nehes.org/resource/resmgr/conference/2026/doc_marcoux_justin.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app should store **both legacy (LS.01.02.01 / EC.02.06.05) and A360 (PE.03.02.01 / PE.xx) references** on checklist templates, because hospital policies and contract specs written before 2026 still cite LS/EC numbers.
- Workflow linkage: PCRA (hospital) → ICRA permit (other researcher) + ILSM assessment → daily ILSM checklists. A change in project phase/scope should force re-assessment (Cleveland Clinic SOP).
- Barriers are the main ILSM/ICRA overlap: a single barrier inspection item should capture both "smoke-tight or noncombustible/limited-combustible" (ILSM) and dust-tight/negative pressure (ICRA), with one photo set satisfying both.

## 7. Most-cited survey findings related to construction life safety

### Takeaway
Fire/life-safety items dominate JC hospital findings: in 2024 the most frequently scored EC/LS EPs were sprinkler-related (LS.02.01.35 EP 4 and EP 14) and fire-rated barrier/penetration items (LS.02.01.10 EP 14 wall penetrations, EP 11 fire-rated walls) — precisely what above-ceiling and barrier work by contractors breaks.

### Cited Findings
- **Jan–Aug 2024 most frequently scored EC/LS EPs:** LS.02.01.35 EP 4 (55.1), LS.02.01.10 EP 14 (48.7), LS.02.01.35 EP 14 (47.4), LS.02.01.10 EP 11 (46.1) (unit not stated in extract; likely % of surveys). — [Mills, 2024 Joint Commission Update (NEHES 10/3/2024)](https://cdn.ymaws.com/nehes.org/resource/resmgr/conference/2024/doc_mills_george100324.pdf)

- **FY2025 (secondary):** EC.02.06.01 and EC.02.05.01 alternated as the first/second most-cited standards, cited in >60% of hospital surveys. — [Accrediculture EC checklist 2026](https://www.accrediculture.com/environment-of-care-joint-commission-checklist/)

- **JC Physical Environment Portal module on LS.02.01.30** (smoke barriers/doors) exists as a JC teaching resource. — [JC PE Portal Module 6, LS.02.01.30](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/5b35ac9e343e455daaedd27f46dbbefa?v=cfcaab72)

### Inferences
- UNVERIFIED background on EP meanings: LS.02.01.35 EP 4 ≈ sprinkler heads not damaged/painted/obstructed and escutcheons/ceiling intact; EP 14 ≈ 18-in clearance below sprinkler deflectors; LS.02.01.10 EP 11 ≈ fire-rated walls maintained; EP 14 ≈ penetrations sealed with approved firestop. If confirmed, the app's close-out checklist for any above-ceiling or wall work should include: ceiling tiles replaced (no gaps that affect sprinkler/smoke performance), sprinkler heads not damaged/painted/obstructed, 18-in storage clearance, every penetration firestopped with a listed system and photographed.
- These top findings are created by trades (MEP, IT/low-voltage) rather than by fire watch lapses, so per-penetration evidence capture is likely the highest-value feature for survey risk.
