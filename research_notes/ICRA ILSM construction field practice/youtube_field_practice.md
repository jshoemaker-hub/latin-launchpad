## 1. How crews set up and maintain ICRA barriers and containment, and what daily inspections look like

### Takeaway
I could not get a single transcript, so I have no field-level wording from videos. Written sources describe the kit and the routine. The kit is hard panels (modular or prefab), coroplast, or fire-retardant poly; negative air machines (HEPA); manometers or differential pressure monitors; taped unused doors; and above-ceiling barriers. The routine is a contractor-completed daily monitoring log that checks barrier integrity, negative pressure at every unsealed opening, filters and discharge hoses, and cleaning. Union training (Carpenters, about 8 to 40 hours) teaches containment, airflow control and particulate readings.

### Cited Findings
- Union curriculum: the Carpenters' ICRA course teaches members "how to contain pathogens, control airflow, take accurate particulate readings, protect patients and work without disrupting hospital operations" — [ConstructConnect Canada, via search](https://canada.constructconnect.com/?p=168938); [Carpenters ICRA page](https://www.carpenters.org/icra-health-care-construction/)
- YouTube description: the Construction ICRA curriculum "was developed by the Carpenters union with leading experts in infection control and public health" for "skilled, journey level carpenters" (metadata only) — [YouTube YJpPofFaCcU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YJpPofFaCcU)
- Barrier materials offered by a restoration contractor: prefab panel systems, coroplast panels, and fire-retardant Visqueen. The vendor says Visqueen suits "low traffic, non-patient care areas" and short-term or emergency containment — [ATI Restoration ICRA Barriers](https://atirestoration.com/commercial/infectious-disease-decontamination/icra-barriers/)
- Vendor install videos show tool-less modular panels forming airtight containment, and a two-person containment-zone install (metadata only) — [EDGE Guard](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8spvupar_ko); [Shield Wall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=md33nCFqIlc)
- Hospital policy (UVA Health): "The Daily Monitoring Log (Appendix B) will be used to document inspections of the Work zone. The Contractor Representative is responsible for completion of the log and immediate corrective actions, when necessary." — [UVA ICRA Policy Guidelines 2022](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)
- UVA: the contractor's responsible person "will confirm negative pressure at all nonsealed openings into the Work site ... and will document negative pressure on the ICRA log." The checklist covers intact barriers (including above-ceiling barriers where required), taped unused doors, negative air unit filters and discharge hoses, daily cleaning, and briefing new contractors on ICRA rules. The form stays posted at the site with the ICRA — [UVA ICRA Policy](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf); [UVA ICRA form](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/InfectionControlRiskAssessment.pdf)
- Pressure targets are inconsistent across sources: UVA cites −0.01 to −0.05 in. w.c. (the search summary rendered it as "−0.1 to −0.5"; units or decimals may be garbled). A vendor checklist cites a minimum of −2.5 Pa. Another vendor cites "−0.02 in. WC or more" for Class IV and V — [UVA](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf); [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/pharmaceuticals/icra-checklist-healthcare-construction-renovation); [Higgins EDU](https://higginsedu.com/blog/icra-2-0)
- One secondary source claims contractors are "typically required to keep a daily checklist completed twice per shift and signed by the site superintendent," and that the ICRA permit, daily checklists and HEPA filter logs must be on site. I could not verify this against a primary hospital document — [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-icra-in-construction-infection-control-explained/)
- Contractor article: a superintendent reviews negative-airflow monitoring logs and ICRA/ILSM controls with project engineers before work proceeds. Infection control planning has to start before demo — [Michuda, "The Hidden Dangers of Healthcare Construction"](https://www.michuda.com/post/the-hidden-dangers-of-healthcare-construction)
- Large GC practice (Barton Malow): individual containment set up in each patient room, with work sequenced around patients. The team joined "daily nursing staff huddles to communicate planned construction activities." Site-specific safety training was mandatory and "additional inspections were implemented" — [Barton Malow projects (OhioHealth, MU Health, Sheltering Arms)](https://www.bartonmalow.com/projects/ohiohealth-healthcare-facility-renovation-projects/); [MU Health Care](https://www.bartonmalow.com/projects/mu-health-care-childrens-hospital/)
- Pressure monitoring hardware is moving to connected devices. Abatement Technologies sells an "ICRA Compliance Package" (HEPA-AIRE/PREDATOR air scrubber, PPM3 differential pressure monitor, HEPA vacuum, containment accessories). Omni IQ G720 offers "TriZone" monitoring (containment, anteroom, ambient), a mobile app and automatic alerts. Spycor Omniguard 6 offers cellular and Wi-Fi monitoring — [Abatement Technologies IC10000PK](https://abatement.com/usa/ic10000pk.html); [Omni IQ](https://omnicleanair.com/products/pressure-monitors/); [Spycor](https://spycorbuilding.com/blog/best-differential-pressure-monitors-for-icra-compliance-in-2026-omniguard-6/)

### Inferences
- The daily ICRA inspection in practice is a short, repeatable checklist: barrier intact (including above the ceiling), doors closed and taped, negative pressure reading at each opening, NAM running with filter OK and exhaust hose OK, walk-off mat and cleaning, and new workers briefed. It maps directly onto mobile checklist items with a numeric pressure field and photo evidence.
- Connected pressure monitors already push alerts to phones. A checklist app should consider importing or recording those readings rather than competing with them.
- Pressure thresholds and units vary by hospital (in. w.c. vs. Pa), so the app needs per-project configurable thresholds.

### Gaps
- No superintendent, foreman or tradesperson wording from videos. Transcripts were inaccessible, and no Reddit or forum threads came up in search.
- No first-hand description of anteroom setup, sticky-mat changes, or how often crews actually read manometers (as opposed to policy frequency).
- ZipWall-specific healthcare content and webinars were not found.

## 2. How ICRA and ILSM tasks are documented today, and who signs and checks

### Takeaway
The documented norm is paper. There is an ICRA permit posted at the barrier, a contractor-completed daily monitoring log, an ILSM daily egress log signed per date, and hourly fire watch entries on a form, all returned to the hospital's safety or facilities office. Digital versions exist mainly as generic inspection-app templates (SafetyCulture) and a few niche platforms. I found no user reviews.

### Cited Findings
- UVA: the contractor representative completes the daily monitoring log, and the form is kept posted at the site with the ICRA — [UVA ICRA Policy](https://www.fm.virginia.edu/docs/fpc/healthsystem/ICRAPolicyGuidelines_202208.pdf)
- VUMC ILSM Daily Egress Inspection Log (rev. 2013) is a monthly sheet: "The signatures below certify that the exits and pathways have been checked on the date indicated," with problems corrected. It goes back to the hospital safety office — [VUMC ILSM Daily Egress Inspection Log](https://www.vumc.org/pdc/sites/vumc.org.pdc/files/public_files/ILSM%20DAILY%20EGRESS%20INSPECTION%20LOG%2011-2013.pdf)
- Fire watch documentation (Connecticut state hospital): "The person responsible for conducting the Fire Watch shall perform a surveillance of the area hourly and document the survey on this form." — [Whiting Forensic Hospital ILSM 2018](https://www.cga.ct.gov/ph/tfs/20190426_CVH%20Whiting%20Task%20Force/20190617_WFH%20Operational%20Procedure%20Manual/2018%20WFH%20Interim%20Life%20Safety%20Measures%20Form%20With%20Appendices.pdf)
- NYU Langone: the hospital safety team inspects ILSM projects, "as a rule, daily unless otherwise specified" (weekly for inactive sites), and reports deficiencies to facilities — [NYU Langone Policy 145](https://nyulangone.org/files/policy-145-ils-program.pdf)
- IHS: daily egress inspection is done during a walk-through with the contractor. ILSM evaluations are "documented using a letter format" — [IHS Construction Guidelines – ILSM](https://www.ihs.gov/sites/oehe/themes/responsive2017/display_objects/documents/handbook/02409.pdf)
- Joint Commission's Life Safety document list includes daily inspection of egress routes under LS.01.02.01 (ILSM) — [Joint Commission LS/EC Document List](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/29635f929b954b6c93beed7ed2a7816b?v=8ca3c82b)
- Northwell: construction projects require a pre-construction risk assessment form "and the ILSM Matrix" — [Northwell PCRA/ICRA form](https://www.northwell.edu/sites/northwell.edu/files/2024-01/pre-construction-risk-assessment.pdf)
- ASHE ICRA 2.0 (announced April 20, 2022) provides two free tools: a Matrix of Precautions and an ICRA Permit. A consultant guide says a permit and approval are required for Class III with Type C work and for every Class IV or V, with the matrix result posted on the permit — [ASHE ICRA 2.0 Toolkit](https://www.ashe.org/icra2); [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- Digital templates exist: an ICRA 2.0 assessment template with an infection prevention sign-off step and a "Spectrum Health ILSM Daily Onsite Checklist" on SafetyCulture — [SafetyCulture ICRA template](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/lwh-icra-assessment); [SafetyCulture ILSM checklist](https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/spectrum-health-ilsm-daily-on-site-checklist)
- Niche platforms: Ruya Compliance attaches the ICRA level and permit photos to the building record, with scoped contractor and inspector portals. iFactory markets ICRA barrier tracking with audit trails (promotional) — [Ruya Compliance](https://ruyacompliance.com/); [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/pharmaceuticals/icra-checklist-healthcare-construction-renovation)
- Surveyors expect a set of records: risk assessments, posted plans and permits, daily inspection logs, impairment and restoration documentation, training records, and fire watch and hot work paperwork — [Vertix Builders ILSM guide](https://www.vertixbuilders.com/news/interim-life-safety-measures-in-hospital-construction-guide/34)

### Inferences
- Roles: the contractor (superintendent or "contractor representative") fills out daily ICRA and egress logs. The infection preventionist signs the permit and approves class changes and barrier removal. Hospital safety or facilities staff do their own rounds and keep the records. An app needs multi-party sign-off and a hand-off of completed logs to the hospital.
- The Joint Commission and survey-readiness angle (show the posted permit plus the log history on demand) is a strong selling point.

### Gaps
- No first-hand account (video or podcast) of crews using phones, photos, binders or apps. I found no app user reviews; the only App Store hit, "ICRA For Construction," is an awareness game with 2 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/icra-for-construction/id1299114197)).
- I couldn't confirm whether Procore or other GC platforms are commonly used for ICRA forms. Nothing turned up.

## 3. What goes wrong in the field and how it is caught and corrected

### Takeaway
The written sources name the expected failures: barrier breaches, loss of negative pressure, blocked exits, panels removed to move equipment, and emergency work done without a pre-construction ICRA meeting. The response is to stop work, notify infection prevention, and document the root cause and corrective action. The illustrative anecdotes are vendor marketing, and I found no practitioner video testimony.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor anecdote (likely illustrative, not documented): a new laborer given only brief instructions removed a barrier panel to move equipment, the zone lost negative pressure within about 20 minutes, and an infection preventionist found the breach — [Oxmaint inspection checklist guide](https://oxmaint.com/industries/healthcare/hospital-construction-safety-inspection-checklist-guide)
- The same vendor ranks barrier breaches next to an OR, low negative-pressure readings and blocked exits as the most urgent items. It cites no surveyor data, and its "$15,000–$50,000 per finding" and "34% audit failure rate" figures are unsourced and should not be used — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/healthcare/hospital-construction-safety-inspection-checklist-guide)
- Breach response in a facility policy: stop work in the affected area, notify infection prevention and clinical leadership, and document the root cause and corrective actions. "Any ILSM failures (barrier breaches, air pressure loss, system failures) must be documented and addressed immediately." — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)
- If a barrier is breached or pressure drops, work may need to stop until fixed — [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-icra-in-construction-infection-control-explained/)
- Barrier takedown: under ICRA 2.0 (as summarized), an IP and an engineering representative inspect before Class III, IV or V precautions are removed. Critical barriers stay until the area is cleaned, and negative air keeps running for a period after dust work ends — [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-matrix-construction-barrier/)
- Real-world pain points from the STARC/ASHE survey (150+ facility managers, IPs and contractors): emergency repairs where an ICRA meeting before work "isn't always practical," areas that can't easily vent outside, unclear guidance for non-acute settings and EDs, and difficulty applying guidance on site. Vendor-sponsored, and the full report is gated — [PSQH: New Data Reveals ICRA 2.0 Implementation Challenges](https://www.psqh.com/analysis/new-data-reveals-icra-2-0-implementation-challenges/); [STARC blog](https://starcsystems.com/blog/icra-2-0-practical-strategies-for-real-world-healthcare-construction-challenges/)
- Contractor-authored list "Avoid These 10 Infection Control Pitfalls in Healthcare Construction" exists, but its contents were not retrieved — [King Project Management](https://kingpm.net/infection-control-pitfalls-in-healthcare-construction/)

### Inferences
- The highest-value app features are: (a) logging a deficiency with photo, location and time; (b) a stop-work and IP-notification trigger; (c) corrective-action closeout with re-inspection; and (d) an "emergency/after-hours work" quick ICRA flow, since emergency work is a named gap.
- Untrained or new workers (the "brief instructions" problem) point to an on-device orientation or acknowledgment step tied to the permit.

### Gaps
- No primary data on how often breaches happen, or on Joint Commission citation frequency for construction ICRA or ILSM.
- No first-person accounts of fire watch lapses, propped doors or open ceiling tiles from videos or podcasts.

## 4. How contractor teams interact with hospital infection prevention and facilities

### Takeaway
Contractor-IP-facilities interaction runs through the pre-construction ICRA/PCRA meeting and permit, daily or periodic hospital rounds alongside contractor logs, coordination with clinical staff (for example, daily nursing huddles), and an IP and engineering inspection before barriers come down. On survey readiness, surveyors tour areas under construction using life safety drawings that mark construction zones. I found no description of the questions surveyors put to contractors.

### Cited Findings
- STARC/ASHE survey: about two-thirds (67%) of respondents had adopted ICRA 2.0. IPs and facilities lead, and most review ICRA plans before major projects — [PSQH](https://www.psqh.com/analysis/new-data-reveals-icra-2-0-implementation-challenges/); [STARC eBook page](https://info.starcsystems.com/ashe-icra2.0-adoption)
- ICRA construction requirements "must be included into the contract documents and implemented during construction" — [HFM, Infection control during construction](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/3867-infection-control-during-construction)
- HFM (Dec 5, 2022, excerpting ASHE's ICRA 2.0 Process Guide): an ICRA is required anytime construction, renovation, maintenance or building-system work creates dust or affects environmental conditions — [HFM](https://www.hfmmagazine.com/articles/4623-conducting-infection-control-risk-assessments-the-right-way)
- APIC text: "the ILSMs will override the infection control risk assessment if impairments are present during any phase of construction" — [APIC Construction & Renovation preview](https://apic.org/Resource_/store/books/preview/SLS9808_Preview.pdf)
- ICRA and ILSM are documented separately even though one project usually triggers both. ILSM plans go to the AHJ and the life-safety officer — [CASRAI ILSM guide](https://casrai.org/guides/interim-life-safety-measures-ilsm); [Healthcare Facility Hub](https://healthcarefacilityhub.org/healthcare-construction-renovation-icra-ilsm-infection-control/)
- GC coordination with clinicians: Barton Malow "joined the daily nursing staff huddles to communicate planned construction activities" — [Barton Malow OhioHealth](https://www.bartonmalow.com/projects/ohiohealth-healthcare-facility-renovation-projects/)
- Joint Commission survey: surveyors review life safety drawings that show "areas under construction" to plan the building tour — [Joint Commission Survey Activity Guide](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/2f21045af9d84f5fbc9bfef10d620469?v=04bccd56)
- ILSM impairment thresholds cited by a contractor guide: 4 hours cumulative in 24 for fire alarm and 10 hours for sprinklers, with AHJ direction taking priority. Verify against the current standard — [Vertix Builders](https://www.vertixbuilders.com/news/interim-life-safety-measures-in-hospital-construction-guide/34)

### Inferences
- The app's natural multi-party model is contractor (creates and fills in), IP (approves and inspects), and facilities or safety (rounds and records), with a "survey mode" that pulls up the active permit, ILSM measures and recent logs for any construction zone.

### Gaps
- No practitioner video or podcast content was captured describing a pre-con meeting, a walk-through or a Joint Commission visit from the contractor side.

## 5. Training and certification context

### Takeaway
The trades' ICRA training is mostly union-led. The Carpenters (UBC/CITF) run an 8-hour awareness course, a 24-hour member course, a 40-hour train-the-trainer course and a CITF "ICRA 620" best-practices course. CPWR runs an 8-hour awareness course. The UA runs an ICRA course for instructors ending in an ASSE ICRA certification exam. ASHE runs ICRA 2.0 train-the-trainer sessions, and AHA offers a Certified Health Care Physical Environment Worker course that includes ILSM. The emphasis is containment, negative air, HEPA filtration, particulate readings and work practices.

### Cited Findings
- UBC program tiers: 40-hour train-the-trainer, 24-hour member course, and 8-hour awareness for other trades and hospital workers — [ConstructConnect Canada](https://canada.constructconnect.com/?p=168938)
- Other crafts, including plumbers and pipefitters, have sent "3,467 of their members through the ICRA 8-Hour Awareness Training" (Indiana-Kentucky-Ohio carpenters council) — [CMW Carpenters](https://www.cmwcarpenters.com/icra-program-continues-to-make-hospital-construction-safer/?lang=es)
- UA-linked course syllabus: limited to UA Instructor Training program graduates. It covers containment, negative air pressure, HEPA filtration and safe work practices, and ends with the ASSE ICRA certification exam — [WCC syllabus UAT 314](https://arts.wccnet.edu/mywcc/faculty-staff/curriculum/course-program-data/syllabi/uat/ms_uat314.pdf)
- CPWR 8-hour ICRA Awareness: containment, negative air, HEPA filtration and work practices. Objectives include defining ICRA, explaining HAIs and describing the four classes of ICRA construction — [CPWR ICRA training](https://www.cpwr.com/training/training-programs/infection-control-risk-assessment-icra/)
- CITF "ICRA 620 – Best Practices in Health Care Construction" (technician training document) — [MWHCEC/CITF PDF](https://www.mwhcec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Technician-Training-ICRA-Best-Practices-in-Health-Care-Construction.pdf)

Tennessee Hospital Association and UBC sessions (2022) for IPs and plant operations leaders — [Heartland Center event](https://heartland.public-health.uiowa.edu/event/construction-icra-infection-control-risk-assessment); [TN Center for Patient Safety](https://www.tnpatientsafety.com/tennessee-hospital-association-and-united-brotherhood-of-carpenters-host-infection-control-risk-assessment-icra-construction-training/)
- ASHE ICRA 2.0 train-the-trainer sessions (2022 annual conference, 2023 PDC Summit) — [ASHE 2022](https://ashe.org/node/681978); [ASHE 2023](https://ashe.org/node/686226)
- AHA "Certified Health Care Physical Environment Worker" virtual course covers construction in health care settings, code compliance and ILSM fundamentals. Next session Nov 12, 2026; $345 member / $435 nonmember — [AHA](https://www.aha.org/node/710873)
- Other certificate programs: Green Advantage PCRA/ICRA certificate; ICRA Solutions FMC workshops; Higgins EDU online ICRA course — [Green Advantage](https://greenadvantage.org/pcra-certificate-program); [ICRA Solutions](https://icrasolutions.com/icra-fmc-workshops-certifications/); [Higgins EDU](https://higginsedu.com/course/icra-iq)
- Employers list ICRA certification as preferred or required for healthcare superintendents. A Duke posting asks the superintendent to "apply for and maintain ICRA guidelines, perform quality control audits, update Life Safety drawings" — [Duke careers](https://careers.duke.edu/job/Durham-Construction-Superintendent-NC-27710/1366301900); [CareerBuilder Tampa](https://www.careerbuilder.com/job-details/construction-superintendent-healthcare-tampa-fl--20155b59-62e8-4b0d-bbb6-f8de8d0b6d67)

### Inferences
- Union-trained carpenters are the core ICRA workforce and a likely early-adopter channel, through training centers and councils' ICRA representatives (for example, an "ICRA Representative" role at the Michigan Regional Council of Carpenters shows up on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mike-jackson-jr-a677a2126/)).
- The app could record worker ICRA card or certificate status and expiry as a gate on the permit sign-in.
